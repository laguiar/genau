## 2. The rule in words

**The Sprachbausteine algorithm (2 steps).** In telc B1 Sprachbausteine Teil 1 and Teil 2, at least 5 of the 20 items are connectors. Often all three options have a similar meaning (e.g. **(a) denn (b) weil (c) deshalb**, or **(a) aber (b) obwohl (c) trotzdem**). Never guess by translation alone! Do this:
1. **Step 1 — Look at the verb of the gapped clause:**
   - Is the conjugated verb at the **very end**? → It **must** be a **Subjunktor** (Column 2: *weil, da, obwohl, dass, wenn, falls, damit, sodass, während, nachdem, bevor, bis, seitdem, sobald, indem*).
   - Is the conjugated verb **immediately after the gap** (`___ [VERB] [subject]`)? → It **must** be a **Verbindungsadverb** in position 1 (Column 3: *deshalb, deswegen, daher, trotzdem, dennoch, dann, danach, außerdem, sonst, stattdessen*).
   - Is there a **subject (or adverb) between the gap and the verb** (`___ [subject] [VERB]`)? → It **must** be a **Konjunktor** in position 0 (Column 1: *und, oder, aber, denn, sondern*).
2. **Step 2 — Check the logical direction (Cause vs Result):**
   - **denn** and **weil / da** introduce the **CAUSE** (*why*): *Die Straße ist nass (result), **weil** es geregnet hat (cause).*
   - **deshalb / deswegen / darum / daher / also / so dass** introduce the **RESULT** (*what follows*): *Es hat geregnet (cause); **deshalb** ist die Straße nass (result).*
   - **obwohl** introduces the **obstacle/cause** (*Obwohl es regnet, gehe ich*); **trotzdem / dennoch / aber** introduces the **unexpected action** (*Es regnet; trotzdem gehe ich*).

**`weil` vs `da`.** Both mean "because" and send the verb to the end. **da** is preferred when the reason is already known or when the reason-clause comes **first** (*Da Sie morgen im Urlaub sind, schicke ich die Datei Ihrem Kollegen*).

**`aber` vs `sondern`.** After a positive clause, "but" is always **aber**. After a negative clause (*nicht / kein*), use **sondern** if you **correct/replace** the negated element ("not A, but rather B": *Der Kurs ist nicht am Montag, **sondern** am Dienstag*), and **aber** if you just add a contrasting fact (*Der Kurs ist nicht billig, **aber** er ist sehr gut*).

> 🇧🇷 **Portuguese contrast** — In Portuguese, *porque (denn/weil)*, *por isso (deshalb)*, *embora (obwohl)* and *mesmo assim (trotzdem)* all put the subject before the verb (*Por isso eu fico / Mesmo assim eu vou*). In German, the three columns have three completely different verb positions. Training your eye to check **where the verb sits** before picking the word is the single highest-yield exam tactic.
> 🇪🇸 **Spanish contrast** — In Spanish, *porque (denn/weil)*, *por eso (deshalb)*, *aunque (obwohl)*, and *sin embargo / a pesar de eso (trotzdem)* all allow `Subject + Verb` right after them. In German, choosing the right connector in telc Sprachbausteine is a **verb-position check first**: verb at the end → Column 2 Subjunktor (`weil, da, obwohl, sodass, damit`); verb right after the gap → Column 3 Adverb (`deshalb, trotzdem, sonst`); subject + verb after the gap → Column 1 Konjunktor (`denn, aber, sondern`). Also notice that Spanish ***sino*** (after negation: *no hoy, **sino** mañana*) is the exact match of German **`sondern`** (*nicht heute, **sondern** morgen*)!
> 🇫🇷 **French contrast** — In French, connectors from all three categories (*car, parce que, c'est pourquoi / mais, bien que, pourtant*) keep `Subject + Verb` order after them. In German, choosing between **denn / weil / deshalb** or **aber / obwohl / trotzdem** in Sprachbausteine is a **word-order test first**: verb at the end → Subjunktor (`weil, da, obwohl`); verb right after the gap → Adverb (`deshalb, trotzdem, sonst`); subject + verb after the gap → Konjunktor (`denn, aber, sondern`).
> 🇬🇧 **English contrast** — English *because / although / therefore / however* all keep `Subject + Verb`. In German, *deshalb* and *trotzdem* behave like fronted adverbs and force **Verb + Subject** (*Deshalb **bleibe ich***, never ~~Deshalb ich bleibe~~).

## 3. Watch out

- Mixing up **Cause** (`weil / denn`) and **Result** (`deshalb / sodass`):
  - ~~Ich bin krank, weil bleibe ich zu Hause~~ → *Ich bin krank, **deshalb** bleibe ich zu Hause* OR *Ich bleibe zu Hause, **weil** ich krank bin.*
- Starting a sentence with `denn`: `denn` can **never** start the first clause; use `Da …` or `Weil …` if you put the reason first.
- Forgetting inversion after `trotzdem, deshalb, sonst, außerdem, danach`: ~~Trotzdem wir hatten Spaß~~ → *Trotzdem **hatten wir** Spaß.*
- Using `aber` instead of `sondern` for a direct correction after *nicht/kein*.
