## 1. The rule in one picture

```
 TWO PARTICIPLES USED AS ADJECTIVES (both take normal Rules A / B / C adjective endings!)

 1. PARTIZIP I  =  INFINITIV + -D  +  adjective ending
    Meaning: ACTIVE & SIMULTANEOUS ("doing X right now" = relative clause in Präsens Active)
    lachen + d     →  das lachen-d-e Kind          = das Kind, das (gerade) lacht
    abfahren + d   →  der abfahren-d-e Zug         = der Zug, der (gerade) abfährt
    steigen + d    →  wegen steigen-d-er Preise    = Preise, die steigen

 2. PARTIZIP II  =  ge-…-t / ge-…-en  +  adjective ending
    Meaning: PASSIVE or COMPLETED BEFORE ("having been done / having happened" = relative clause in Passiv or Perfekt)
    reparieren     →  das reparier-t-e Auto        = das Auto, das repariert wurde / ist
    schreiben      →  der geschrieben-e Brief      = der Brief, der geschrieben wurde
    ankommen (sein)→  der angekommen-e Zug         = der Zug, der angekommen ist (completed change/arrival)

 3. EXTENDED PARTICIPIAL PHRASE IN B1 READING (Linksattribut — everything sits between Article and Noun!)
    [ Article ]   [ extra info: time / place / agent ]   [ Partizip I or II + ending ]   [ NOUN ]
      der           gestern von Ihnen                      geschickt-e                     Brief
    = der Brief, der gestern von Ihnen geschickt wurde
      die           ab nächster Woche                      geltend-en                      Regeln
    = die Regeln, die ab nächster Woche gelten
```

## 2. The rule in words

**Why this topic is essential for telc B1 Lesen.** Formal German and newspaper articles love packing a whole relative clause in front of the noun to save space (*die gestern vom Parlament beschlossene Reform*). Once you know the two participle signals, you can read these blocks effortlessly and use short ones in your own letters (*der unterschriebene Vertrag, am kommenden Montag*).

**1. Partizip I (`Infinitiv + -d + adjective ending`).**
- **How to build it:** Take the full infinitive of any verb and add **`-d`** (the only irregular one in the language is *sein → **seiend***, which is rare):
  - *lachen → lachen**d**, schlafen → schlafen**d**, fehlen → fehlen**d**, passen → passen**d**, folgen → folgen**d**, kommen → kommen**d**, dringend, spannend*.
- **What it means:** Always **active** and **ongoing / simultaneous** with the main verb. *Die **wartenden** Fahrgäste* = the passengers who are waiting.
- **High-frequency B1 letter words that are actually Partizip I:** *am **kommenden** Freitag* (this coming Friday), *in der **folgenden** Woche* (in the following week), *ein **passender** Termin* (a suitable appointment), *die **fehlenden** Unterlagen* (the missing documents), *ein **dringendes** Problem* (an urgent problem).

**2. Partizip II (`ge-…-t / ge-…-en + adjective ending`).**
- **How to build it:** Take the normal Perfekt Partizip II and add the adjective ending directly to `-t` or `-en`: *gekocht → das gekocht**e** Ei; gebrochen → ein gebrochen**er** Arm; bestellt → die bestellt**e** Ware*.
- **What it means:** Usually **passive** and **completed** (*die **bezahlte** Rechnung* = the bill that has been paid), or — with `sein` verbs of change/movement — **completed active** (*die **abgefahrenen** Züge* = the trains that have departed; *das **eingeschlafene** Kind*).

**3. How to read extended participial phrases (`Partizipialattribute`).**
When you see an article (*der / die / das / ein*) followed by adverbs or prepositional phrases and then a participle right before a noun:
1. Read the **Article + Noun** at the very end first (*der … Brief*).
2. Turn the **Participle + extra words** in the middle into a relative clause:
   - Partizip I (`-end-`) → active relative clause: *die **in Berlin lebenden** Künstler* = *die Künstler, **die in Berlin leben***.
   - Partizip II → passive (or Perfekt) relative clause: *das **von uns gebuchte** Zimmer* = *das Zimmer, **das von uns gebucht wurde***.

> 🇧🇷 **Portuguese contrast** — - Partizip I (`-d`) corresponds to PT `-nte` adjectives (*seguinte → folgend-, urgente → dringend-*) or a relative clause *que está fazendo* (*die schlafenden Kinder* = *as crianças que estão dormindo*). Never use Partizip I for a continuous tense after *sein* (~~Ich bin arbeitend~~ does NOT exist!). - Partizip II corresponds directly to PT past participles used as adjectives (*a conta paga* → *die bezahlte Rechnung*, *o contrato assinado* → *der unterschriebene Vertrag*), except in German the participle + all its modifiers stand **before** the noun!
> 🇪🇸 **Spanish contrast** — Spanish *participio pasado* used as an adjective (*el contrato firmado, la mercancía entregada*) corresponds to German **Partizip II + adjective ending** (*der unterschrieben**e** Vertrag, die geliefert**e** Ware*), while active ongoing descriptions (*los niños que ríen / el próximo lunes / la semana siguiente*) use **Partizip I** (`Infinitiv + -d + ending`: *die lachend**en** Kinder, am kommend**en** Montag, in der folgend**en** Woche*). In B1 reading texts, remember that German puts the entire modifier **before** the noun (*el correo enviado ayer por usted → der **gestern von Ihnen geschickte** Brief*).
> 🇫🇷 **French contrast** — French *participe présent* (`-ant`: *les enfants souriants, la semaine suivante*) matches German **Partizip I** (`Infinitiv + -d + adjective ending`: *die lächelnd**en** Kinder, in der folgend**en** Woche*), and French *participe passé* (*le contrat signé*) matches **Partizip II + adjective ending** (*der unterschrieben**e** Vertrag*). Crucial reading difference: French puts extended participial phrases **after** the noun (*le courrier **envoyé hier par votre service***); German packs the entire phrase **between the article and the noun** (*der **gestern von Ihnen geschickte** Brief*).
> 🇬🇧 **English contrast** — English `-ing` before a noun (*the sleeping child, rising prices*) matches German Partizip I (`-end`). English past participle (*the stolen car*) matches Partizip II (`gestohlen-`). Unlike English (*the letter sent yesterday*), German puts the whole phrase **before** the noun (*der gestern geschickte Brief*).

## 3. Watch out

- Using Partizip I after *sein* like English `-ing`: ~~Er ist schlafend~~ → *Er **schläft**.* (Partizip I is only used **before a noun** or as an adverb of manner: *Er kam **lachend** ins Zimmer*).
- Forgetting the `-d` of Partizip I (making it look like an adjective/infinitive): ~~am kommen Freitag~~ → *am kommen**den** Freitag*.
- Confusing active (`-end`) and passive (Partizip II):
  - *die **suchenden** Eltern* (the parents who are searching) vs *die **gesuchten** Personen* (the people who are being searched for).
