## 1. The rule in one picture

```
 AFTER der / die / das / dieser / jeder / welcher / alle / mancher / solcher   →   only TWO endings: -e or -en

           m              n              f              pl
 ●   der  alt E  Mann    das  alt E  Kind    die  alt E  Frau    die  alt EN Leute
 ▲   den  alt EN Mann    das  alt E  Kind    die  alt E  Frau    die  alt EN Leute
 ■   dem  alt EN Mann    dem  alt EN Kind    der  alt EN Frau    den  alt EN Leuten
 (◆  des  alt EN Mannes  des  alt EN Kindes  der  alt EN Frau    der  alt EN Leute   — B1-01)

 THE PICTURE:  a box of -e in the top-left (● m/n/f and ▲ n/f  = 5 cells) — everything else is -en.
               "-e where the article is der/das/die and the case is ● (or ▲ with das/die); -en everywhere else"

 several adjectives → all get the same ending:  der kleine, alte, schöne Garten
```

## 2. The rule in words

**Why there is an ending at all.** Before a noun, an adjective must show the noun's case, gender and number **once**. If the article already shows it clearly (*der, den, dem, das, die*), the adjective can relax and take a weak ending: **-e** or **-en**. That is the whole "Rule A" (see [shared/cases.md §8](../shared/cases.md)).

**Which one.** *-e* in five cells: ● masculine (*der alte Mann*), ● and ▲ neuter (*das kleine Kind*), ● and ▲ feminine (*die junge Frau*). *-en* everywhere else: ▲ masculine (*den alten Mann*), all ■ (*dem alten Mann, dem kleinen Kind, der jungen Frau*), all plural (*die alten Leute, den alten Leuten*), and later all ◆.

**Same after `dieser`, `jeder`, `welcher`, `alle`, `mancher`, `solcher`.** They carry the same endings as *der*, so the adjective behaves the same: *dieser neue Kollege, jedes kleine Kind, welche rote Jacke, alle alten Häuser, mit diesem alten Auto*.

**Spelling adjustments.** *hoch → hohe* (*das hohe Haus*), *dunkel → dunkle*, *teuer → teure*, *sauer → saure*; adjectives from cities take *-er* and never decline: *der Berliner Bahnhof*. Colours and *super, prima, rosa, lila* from foreign words do not decline: *das rosa Kleid*.

**Predicative stays bare** (A1-18): *Der Mann ist alt* / *der alte Mann*.

> 🇧🇷 **Portuguese contrast** — PT adjectives agree in gender and number (*o homem velho, a mulher velha, os homens velhos*), so agreement itself is familiar; the differences are position (German **before** the noun, always) and that the ending encodes **case**, not just gender. There is no PT feel for *-e* vs *-en*; learn the picture (5 cells of *-e*).
> 🇪🇸 **Spanish contrast** — In Spanish, descriptive adjectives usually follow the noun (*el coche nuevo, la casa grande*) and their ending (`-o/-a/-os/-as`) only depends on gender and number. In German, attributive adjectives **always precede the noun** and their ending depends on the **determiner** before them and the **case**: after `der / die / das / dieser / jeder / welcher / alle` (Rule A), the ending is **`-e`** in only 5 cells (`●` m/n/f, `▲` n/f) and **`-en`** in every other cell.
> 🇫🇷 **French contrast** — In French, most descriptive adjectives stand **after** the noun (*une voiture rouge, un livre intéressant*) and agree only in gender and number. In German, attributive adjectives **always stand before the noun** and carry a case ending. After `der/die/das` (and `dieser, jeder, welcher, alle`), only two endings exist: **`-e`** in the 5 basic cells (`●` m/n/f, `▲` n/f) and **`-en`** everywhere else.
> 🇬🇧 **English contrast** — Position is the same as English (*the old man*); the ending is new. English speakers tend to write *der alt Mann* — no ending at all is always wrong before a noun.

## 3. Watch out

- ▲ masculine is the cell everyone forgets: *Ich sehe **den alten** Mann* (both *-en*).
- ■ is *-en* for **all** genders: *mit dem neuen Auto, mit der neuen Kollegin, mit den neuen Kollegen*.
- Plural ● and ▲ after *die* is *-en*, not *-e*: *die kleinen Kinder*.
- Ordinal numbers are adjectives: *der erste Tag, am ersten Tag, im dritten Stock*.
- *Welch-* and *jed-* themselves take *der*-endings: *welchen alten Mann, jedem kleinen Kind*.
