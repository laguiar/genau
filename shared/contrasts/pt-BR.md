# Language Pack — Brazilian Portuguese (`pt-BR`)

**Code:** `pt-BR` · **Language:** Brazilian Portuguese · **Flag:** 🇧🇷 · **Label:** Portuguese contrast

Master reference for Brazilian Portuguese speakers learning German up to telc B1. `python3 review/review.py lang sync` uses the `## Topic notes` section below to populate the `> 🇧🇷 **Portuguese contrast**` boxes across `A1/`, `A2/`, and `B1/`.

## Nouns and articles

| German feature | Portuguese | English | Note |
|----------------|-----------|---------|------|
| Three genders, arbitrary | helps (2 genders, arbitrary: *a mesa*) | misleads (no gender) | Learn every noun **with** `der/die/das` like you learned *o/a*. But gender does not transfer: *o sol* → **die** Sonne, *a lua* → **der** Mond. |
| Article agreement | helps (*o/os/a/as*) | misleads | Articles change for **case** too, not only gender/number. |
| Noun capitalisation | misleads | misleads | Every noun gets a capital letter. Check this last in every text. |
| Plural: many patterns, umlaut | neither | neither | Learn plural with the noun: `der Mann, ¨-er`. |
| Compound nouns | neither (PT uses *de*: *mesa de jantar*) | helps a little (*dinner table*) | Last part decides gender: *das Esszimmer* ← *das Zimmer*. |

## Cases

| German feature | Portuguese | English | Note |
|----------------|-----------|---------|------|
| Four cases on articles/adjectives/pronouns | neither (PT only has case in pronouns: *eu/me/mim*) | neither (*I/me*) | Use the pronoun intuition you already have (*me/mim*, *me/him*) as the seed: Akkusativ ≈ *me*, Dativ ≈ *(para) mim / lhe*. |
| Dativ for indirect object | helps (*Dei-**lhe** o livro*) | helps (*I gave **him** the book*) | Dativ verbs without a "to" (`helfen, danken, gefallen`) mislead in both. |
| Genitiv = possession | helps (*o carro **do** meu pai*) | helps (*my father's car*) | German uses `von + ■` in speech and ◆ in writing. |

## Verbs

| German feature | Portuguese | English | Note |
|----------------|-----------|---------|------|
| Person endings (`-e, -st, -t, -en`) | helps (PT conjugates fully) | misleads (EN barely conjugates) | You already track person on the verb; only the endings are new. |
| `du` vs `Sie` | helps (*você* vs *o senhor / a senhora*) | misleads | `Sie` takes 3rd person plural form, like *o senhor* takes 3rd person. |
| Perfekt with `haben/sein` | misleads (*pretérito perfeito* is simple) | helps (*I have eaten*) — but German uses it for finished past too | German Perfekt = spoken/informal past; Präteritum = written narrative. PT *perfeito* covers both. |
| `sein` verbs (movement/change) | neither | neither | *Ich **bin** gefahren* — no PT/EN equivalent; memorise the class. |
| Modal verbs + infinitive at end | helps (*posso/devo/quero* + infinitive) | helps (*can/must/want*) | New: the infinitive goes to the **end** of the clause. |
| `müssen nicht` = not necessary | misleads (*não deve* = must not) | misleads (*must not* = prohibited) | Prohibition = `nicht dürfen`. |
| Konjunktiv II (`hätte, wäre, würde`) | helps (*teria, seria, faria*; *se eu tivesse*) | helps (*would, had*) | Map: `würde + Inf.` ≈ *-ria*; `wenn ich … hätte` ≈ *se eu tivesse*. |
| Passiv with `werden` | helps (*é feito / foi feito*) | helps (*is made*) | But PT often prefers *se* passive (*vende-se*) ≈ German `man`. |
| Reflexive verbs | helps (*lembrar-se, sentar-se*) | misleads | Same idea as PT; which verbs are reflexive differs. |
| Separable verbs | neither | helps a little (*get up, call back*) | The prefix goes to the **end**: *Ich rufe dich **an**.* |
| Präsens for future | helps (*Amanhã eu viajo*) | helps (*I'm leaving tomorrow*) | `werden` is optional at B1. |

## Word order

| German feature | Portuguese | English | Note |
|----------------|-----------|---------|------|
| Verb second (V2): whatever is first, verb comes second | misleads | misleads | *Morgen **fahre** ich* — not *Morgen ich fahre*. The most frequent writing error. |
| Verb final in subordinate clauses | misleads | misleads | *…, weil ich keine Zeit **habe**.* Colloquial German breaks this after `weil`; the exam does not. |
| Yes/no question = verb first | misleads (PT uses intonation) | helps (*Do you…?* → *Kommst du?*) | No `do`-support: the main verb itself moves. |
| Time – Manner – Place | misleads (PT: place then time) | misleads | *Ich fahre **morgen mit dem Zug nach Berlin**.* |
| `nicht` position | misleads (*não* before the verb) | misleads | `nicht` goes after the verb and objects, before the part it negates. |
| Dativ before Akkusativ (nouns); pronoun before noun | neither | neither | *Ich gebe **dem Kind** **den Ball**.* / *Ich gebe **ihn** dem Kind.* |
| Adjective before the noun | misleads (*casa branca*) | helps (*white house*) | And it takes an ending. |

## Spelling and sounds relevant to writing

| German | Note |
|--------|------|
| `ß` vs `ss` | `ß` after a long vowel or diphthong (*Straße, heißen*); `ss` after a short vowel (*essen, Fluss*). |
| Umlauts | `ä ö ü` are letters, not accents; `ae oe ue` only when the keyboard has none. |
| `ie` vs `ei` | *ie* = long "ee" (*Liebe*); *ei* = "eye" (*mein*). PT speakers reverse these. |
| Double consonants | Signal a short vowel (*kommen, bitte*). PT rarely uses them. |
| Capital `Sie/Ihnen/Ihr` in letters | The formal pronoun and its forms are always capitalised. `du` is not. |
| No accents | German has no *á/ã/ç*. Only umlauts and `ß`. |

## Vocabulary transfer

- **Latin cognates PT→DE** are frequent in formal/written German: *Information, Universität, Situation, Kultur, Familie, Musik, Natur, Problem, Zentrum, Konzert, Termin (≠ término)*. Helps in Lesen. · **False friends PT/DE:** *Gymnasium* (secondary school), *Termin* (appointment), *bekommen* (receive, not *become*), *Mappe* (folder), *Kompromiss* fine, *Präservativ* (condom, as PT *preservativo*), *Chef* (boss, not cook), *aktuell* (current), *eventuell* (possibly, not *eventually*). · **Germanic cognates EN→DE:** *Haus, Wasser, Brot, trinken, kommen, Hand, Finger, alt, gut, kalt*. Helps in Lesen; beware *Gift* (poison), *bekommen*, *also* (so), *fast* (almost), *Rat* (advice), *wo* (where, not who).

## Topic notes

### A1-01
Portuguese never capitalises common nouns and has no double-consonant length rule, so both errors are automatic for a PT speaker. Also: German has **no accents** (*á, ã, ç*) and `ç`-like "ss" sounds are written `ss/ß`. PT speakers reverse `ie/ei` because PT *ei* is "ay" (*lei*) — the German *ei* is closer to PT *ai* (*pai*).

### A1-02
You already conjugate for person (*moro, moras, mora, moramos, moram*), so the idea is familiar; only the endings are new. `Sie` works like *o senhor / a senhora*: the polite form takes a 3rd-person shape. Unlike PT, **the pronoun is never dropped**: *Moro em Berlim* → ***Ich** wohne in Berlin*. Present continuous: PT *estou aprendendo* → just *ich lerne*.

### A1-03
PT also has stem changes in the same slots (*dormir → durmo; preferir → prefiro*), so the phenomenon is familiar. But **`sein` covers both *ser* and *estar***: *Ich bin Lehrer* (ser) / *Ich bin müde* (estar) / *Ich bin in Berlin* (estar). Do not look for an *estar*-verb. And **`haben` is *ter*, not *haver***: *Ich habe Zeit / Hunger / ein Auto*. "There is" is *es gibt* (A1-18), not *haben*.

### A1-04
Portuguese keeps the verb after the subject even when something else comes first: *Hoje eu trabalho.* German must flip: *Heute **arbeite ich**.* And PT yes/no questions look like statements (*Você mora em Berlim?*); German moves the verb to the front: ***Wohnst** du in Berlin?* Question words are close to PT (*onde* = wo, *de onde* = woher, *para onde* = wohin, *quando* = wann, *quem* = wer, *o que* = was, *como* = wie, *por que* = warum).

### A1-05
You already learn nouns with *o/a*, so keep the habit. But PT gender is **not** a guide: *o sol → die Sonne, a lua → der Mond, o carro → das Auto, a mesa → der Tisch, o livro → das Buch, a casa → das Haus*. Trust the German ending, never the PT word. Like PT, professions take no article: *Sou professor* = *Ich bin Lehrer* (English *I am **a** teacher* misleads).

### A1-06
PT plural is almost always *-s*; the German *-s* plural is the **rarest** pattern, reserved for foreign words. PT speakers over-use it (~~die Kinds~~). Also PT *os/as* → one German plural article, *die*.

### A1-07
PT has one word, *não*, placed **before** the verb (*Não tenho carro, não moro em Berlim*). German has two words and puts *nicht* **after** the verb. And PT double negation (*não vi ninguém*) is not German: *Ich habe **niemanden** gesehen* (one negative). PT *nenhum/nenhuma* ≈ *kein/keine* — a useful anchor: if you would say *nenhum(a)* or *não … um(a)*, use *kein*.

### A1-08
PT marks the object only on pronouns (*Eu o vejo / Vejo-o*; *me, te, nos*). Map: *me* → *mich*, *te* → *dich*, *o* → *ihn*, *a* → *sie*, *nos* → *uns*, *vos/vocês* → *euch*. The new part: **articles** change too, and only for masculine. PT object pronouns go before the verb (*Eu te amo*); German puts them **after** the conjugated verb: *Ich liebe dich.*

### A1-09
PT *seu/sua* is famously ambiguous (his, her, their, your); *dele/dela* fixes it. German always distinguishes: *dele* = *sein-*, *dela* = *ihr-*, *deles/delas* = *ihr-*. PT agrees the possessive with the owned noun (*seu carro, sua casa*) — same principle as the German ending, so this feels natural; the new part is the case ending (*-en*).

### A1-10
PT has no separable verbs; prefixes never move (*re-fazer, des-ligar*). The closest feeling is a verb + adverb pair (*ligar de volta*, *sair fora*), where the adverb goes to the end — that is the German pattern, only obligatory.

### A1-11
*posso / preciso (tenho que) / quero / gostaria (queria) / gosto de* map cleanly to *kann / muss / will / möchte / mag*. Two differences: the infinitive goes to the **end** (*Preciso comprar pão hoje* → *Ich muss heute Brot **kaufen***), and *gostar de + verb* is not *mögen* + verb but *gern* + verb.

### A1-12
PT imperatives change form for *tu/você/vocês/o senhor* too, and the polite form also uses a "different" conjugation (*venha, tome*), so the three-way system feels natural. Two differences: German drops the pronoun for *du/ihr* but **keeps** it for *Sie* after the verb (*Kommen Sie!*), and negative imperatives simply add *nicht* (*Komm nicht!*) instead of a special form (*não venhas*).

### A1-13
PT says tens first (*vinte e quatro*); German says units first (*vierundzwanzig*) — the most frequent slip when writing numbers you hear. *halb neun*: PT *oito e meia* = 8:30 = *halb **neun*** (half **to** nine). PT *de manhã / à tarde / à noite* → *am Morgen / am Nachmittag / am Abend* but *in der Nacht*. PT *em maio* → *im Mai*; *na segunda* → *am Montag*; *às oito* → *um acht*.

### A1-14
*para/a* Berlim → *nach*; *em* Berlim → *in*; *de* Berlim → *aus*. *Vou ao médico / à padaria* → *zum Arzt / zum Bäcker* (**zu**, not *nach*). *Estou na casa da minha avó* → *bei meiner Oma* (**bei**, not *in*). *de ônibus* → *mit dem Bus* (**mit**). *em casa / para casa* → *zu Hause / nach Hause* — note that *zu* here means location, the one exception.

### A1-15
PT *lhe / me / te / nos* and *para mim / para ele* are Dativ-like: *Isso me agrada* → *Das gefällt mir*; *Ajudo-o* → but German *helfen* takes ■: *Ich helfe **ihm**.* Verbs that are direct in PT but ■ in German: *ajudar (helfen), agradecer (danken), responder (antworten), acreditar em alguém (glauben), parabenizar (gratulieren), pertencer (gehören)*. *Gostar de X* is best rendered *X gefällt mir* for things you see, *mögen* for general liking.

### A1-16
PT uses the **simple** past (*comi, fui*) where German uses the Perfekt. Do **not** map Perfekt onto PT *tenho comido* (which means "I've been eating lately"). *Ich habe gegessen* = *comi*. *ser/ir* → *bin gewesen / bin gegangen* — the *sein* auxiliary has no PT parallel; memorise the movement/change list.

### A1-17
*era/estava, tinha, podia, precisava (tinha que), queria* are imperfeito forms, and the German Präteritum here matches them in feel. But PT distinguishes *era* (imperfeito) from *foi* (perfeito); German *war* covers both. *Havia / tinha* (there was) → *es gab*.

### A1-18
PT adjectives after *ser/estar* agree (*a casa é grande, as casas são grandes*); German predicative adjectives never change. PT adverbs take *-mente*; German takes nothing. *gostar de fazer* → verb + *gern*; *preferir* → *lieber*; *o que eu mais gosto* → *am liebsten*. *há / tem um parque* → *es gibt einen Park* (▲, and *gibt* is 3rd sg. of *geben*). *muito* splits into *sehr* (muito grande) and *viel* (trabalha muito).

### A1-19
PT default is place before time (*Vou a Berlim amanhã*); German reverses it (*Ich fahre morgen nach Berlin*). PT *e, ou, mas, pois/porque* → *und, oder, aber, denn*; behave the same (no verb move). PT *depois / então* → *dann / danach*, **but** they trigger inversion in German: *Depois eu vou* → *Dann gehe ich*.

### A1-20
PT uses *ele/ela* for objects too (*a mesa? ela está aqui*), so grammatical-gender pronouns feel natural — just remember the German gender, not the PT one. *a gente / se* (*fala-se alemão*) → *man*. PT drops dummy subjects (*Chove. Está frio.*); German needs *es*. *nada/ninguém* with *não* is double negation in PT; German uses one word: *Ich sehe niemanden. Ich habe nichts.*

### A2-01
PT indirect objects use *lhe/lhes* or *para/a + pessoa*: *Dei o livro **ao** menino / Dei-**lhe** o livro.* The German ■ does the job of both *a/para* and *lhe*, with no preposition: *Ich gab **dem** Jungen das Buch.* PT clitic order (*Dei-lho*) is roughly "■ + ▲" but German pronoun order is the reverse: *Ich gab **es ihm**.* Verbs that are direct in PT but ■ in German: *ajudar, agradecer, responder, seguir, acreditar em, obedecer, parabenizar, pertencer a, faltar a, doer (dói-me = tut mir weh)*.

### A2-02
PT marks the difference with prepositions (*em cima da mesa* vs *para cima da mesa*; *no cinema* vs *ao cinema*); German keeps one preposition and switches **case**. PT uses one verb *pôr/colocar* and *estar*; German needs *stellen/legen/setzen/hängen* by shape and *stehen/liegen/sitzen/hängen* — this is new, memorise the pairs. *sentar-se* → *sich setzen* (▲, reflexive) and *estar sentado* → *sitzen*.

### A2-03
*para* is split: benefit → *für* (▲), direction → *zu/nach* (■). *Há três anos que moro aqui* → *seit drei Jahren* + present tense. *de* is split: origin → *aus*, from-a-person/event → *von*, possession → *von* + ■ or Genitiv. *com* → *mit* + ■ always (*com ele* → *mit ihm*). *sem* → *ohne* + ▲ (*sem mim* → *ohne mich*). *por* (through) → *durch*; *contra* → *gegen*.

### A2-04
*Moro aqui há três anos* → ***seit** drei Jahren* + present (not Perfekt). *Cheguei há três anos* → ***vor** drei Jahren* + past. *daqui a uma hora* → ***in** einer Stunde*. *desde 2020* → *seit 2020*. *até sexta* → *bis Freitag*. *a partir de amanhã* → *ab morgen*. *durante* → *während*. *só às 10* → *erst um 10*. PT *ainda não / já não* → *noch nicht / nicht mehr*.

### A2-05
*-ieren* verbs are mostly Latin cognates (*estudar, reparar, telefonar, organizar, funcionar, reservar*): easy to recognise, just remember no *ge-*. The *haben/sein* split has no PT equivalent; the object test (*Dirigi o carro* → *habe … gefahren*; *Fui de carro* → *bin … gefahren*) is the reliable rule.

### A2-06
PT has one simple past for speech and writing (*fui, comi*); German splits: spoken/letters = Perfekt, narrative prose = Präteritum. Reading a German news text in the Präteritum feels like the PT *pretérito perfeito*; do not assume the imperfeito nuance. Many strong-verb vowel changes look like PT irregulars (*fazer – fiz; ter – tive*) — same kind of memorisation.

### A2-07
PT *poder* covers both *können* (ability) and *dürfen* (permission); German separates them (*Posso nadar* → *Ich kann schwimmen*; *Posso fumar aqui?* → *Darf ich hier rauchen?*). *não pode* (prohibition) → *nicht dürfen*; *não precisa* → *nicht müssen*. *dever* (should, according to someone) → *sollen*; *ter que* → *müssen*. PT *devo?* in offers → *Soll ich …?*

### A2-08
PT adjectives agree in gender and number (*o homem velho, a mulher velha, os homens velhos*), so agreement itself is familiar; the differences are position (German **before** the noun, always) and that the ending encodes **case**, not just gender. There is no PT feel for *-e* vs *-en*; learn the picture (5 cells of *-e*).

### A2-09
Nothing in PT prepares you for endings changing depending on *o/um/nada*; treat the "signal once" idea as a puzzle rule, not a translation. But the letter formulas can be learned as chunks, exactly as *Atenciosamente* is.

### A2-10
PT uses *mais + adjetivo + (do) que* for almost everything; German always uses the *-er* suffix (*mais interessante* → *interessanter*, never *mehr interessant*). *tão … quanto/como* → *so … wie*; *do que* → *als*. Irregulars map: *melhor* → *besser*, *maior* → *größer* (regular in German), *mais* → *mehr*. *o mais alto* → *der höchste / am höchsten*.

### A2-11
PT *que / porque / se-quando* keep normal order (*Acho que ele vem amanhã*); German moves the verb: *Ich glaube, dass er morgen **kommt**.* The comma before *dass/weil/wenn* is obligatory in German, unlike PT before *que*. *se* (if) and *quando* (whenever) both → *wenn*; *quando* for a single past event → *als* (later). PT drops the subject in the subordinate (*porque estou cansado*); German keeps it: *weil **ich** müde bin*.

### A2-12
*Não sei se ele vem* → *ob* (not *wenn*!) — PT *se* means both *if* and *whether*; German splits them: condition = *wenn*, whether = *ob*. *Não sei quando ele vem* → *wann* (question word) — never *wenn*. Purpose: *para que + subjuntivo* → *damit*; *para + infinitivo* → *um … zu*. Same subject test works exactly like PT (*para eu trabalhar* → *damit ich arbeite* is also possible, but *um zu arbeiten* is more natural).

### A2-13
Map *würde + Inf.* onto the *futuro do pretérito* (*-ria*): *Eu compraria* → *Ich würde kaufen*; *Poderia me ajudar?* → *Könnten Sie mir helfen?*; *Eu gostaria de …* → *Ich hätte gern / Ich würde gern*; *Seria ótimo* → *Das wäre toll*; *Você deveria dormir mais* → *Du solltest mehr schlafen*. This mapping is nearly one-to-one — use it.

### A2-14
PT is rich in reflexives (*lembrar-se, sentar-se, esquecer-se, queixar-se*), so the idea is familiar; but the lists differ: *esquecer-se* → *vergessen* (not reflexive), *lembrar-se de* → *sich erinnern an*, *pressa: apressar-se* → *sich beeilen*, *divertir-se* → *sich amüsieren*, *casar-se* → *heiraten* (not reflexive), *tornar-se* → *werden*. The ■ reflexive (*Ich wasche mir die Hände*) matches PT *lavo as mãos* only in meaning; PT has no dative reflexive. Prepositions are the big trap: *pensar em* → *denken an ▲*, *esperar por* → *warten auf ▲*, *interessar-se por* → *sich interessieren für ▲*, *ter medo de* → *Angst haben vor ■*, *sonhar com* → *träumen von ■*, *falar sobre/de* → *sprechen über ▲ / von ■*, *cuidar de* → *sich kümmern um ▲*.

### A2-15
PT uses one word, *que*, for everything (*o homem que mora aqui / o homem que eu vi*); German makes you decide gender, number **and** case. Use the PT test: if you could replace *que* by *ele/ela/eles* → ●; by *o/a/os/as* (object) → ▲. Verb goes to the end, unlike PT. *onde* → *wo* works the same way.

### A2-16
*por isso / portanto / então / mesmo assim / depois / além disso / senão* → *deshalb / also / dann / trotzdem / danach / außerdem / sonst* — meanings map well. The difference is purely word order: *Por isso eu fico em casa* → *Deshalb **bleibe ich** zu Hause*. PT *então* is also used as a filler; German *also* is used the same way in speech but in writing keep it for "so".

### A2-17
*o meu / a minha* standing alone → *meiner/meins/meine* (with case!). *este / esse* → *dieser*; *qual?* → *welcher?* (choosing) and *que tipo de?* → *was für ein?* — PT often uses *qual* for both, so decide by meaning. *alguém / ninguém* → *jemand / niemand* (no double negation: *Ninguém veio* → *Niemand ist gekommen*). *alguns / vários / poucos* → *einige / mehrere / wenige* (declined).

### A2-18
PT drops the article with professions (*Sou médico*) like German. Two traps: PT uses articles with countries (*o Brasil, a Alemanha*), German does not (*Brasilien, Deutschland*); PT *muito/pouco* agree (*muitos amigos, pouca água*) which matches *viele/wenig* but the German uncountable forms are **bare** (*wenig Wasser*). *todo/toda* → *jeder* (each) vs *todos* → *alle* (all): *todo dia* = *jeden Tag*, *todos os dias* = *alle Tage / jeden Tag*. *alguns / vários / poucos / um pouco de* → *einige / mehrere / wenige / etwas*.

### A2-19
*ficar, tornar-se, virar* (become) all → *werden*: *ficou tarde* → *es wurde spät*; *virou médico* → *er wurde Arzt*; *estou ficando cansado* → *ich werde müde*. PT future *vou viajar / viajarei* → German usually **Präsens + time word** (*Ich reise morgen*); *werden* only for promises/predictions. *ser/estar + particípio* (passive: *é feito*) → *wird gemacht*.

### A2-20
PT places clitic pronouns before the verb (*Ele me deu o livro / Eu me lavo*); German puts them **after** the conjugated verb. PT order *deu-lhe o livro* (■ pronoun, ▲ noun) matches *gab ihm das Buch*; but *deu-**mo*** (■ + ▲ pronouns) is the reverse of German *gab **es mir*** (▲ + ■). PT adverbials: place before time (*Vou a Berlim amanhã de trem*) → German *morgen mit dem Zug nach Berlin*. *não* before the verb → *nicht* late in the sentence.

### A2-21
Two-object verbs: PT *dar/mostrar/explicar algo a alguém* → German no *a*: *jemandem etwas geben*. The *da-*compounds have no PT form; PT repeats the preposition + pronoun (*Penso nisso / Espero por isso*) — German fuses: *daran, darauf*. *Em que você pensa?* → *Woran denkst du?*; *Com quem?* → *Mit wem?*. Preposition mismatches to memorise: *agradecer por* → *sich bedanken für*, *pedir algo* → *bitten um*, *participar de* → *teilnehmen an ■*, *reclamar de* → *sich beschweren über ▲*, *depender de* → *abhängen von*, *concordar com* → *einverstanden sein mit*, *preparar-se para* → *sich vorbereiten auf ▲*, *acostumar-se com* → *sich gewöhnen an ▲*, *apaixonar-se por* → *sich verlieben in ▲*.

### B1-01
Portuguese *de / do / da / dos / das* maps straight to Genitiv ◆: *a casa **do** professor* → *das Haus **des** Lehrers*; *por causa **da** chuva* → *wegen **des** Regens*; *durante **a** viagem* → *während **der** Reise*. Remember that for m/n singular both the article **and** the noun get `-s`: *des Lehrer**s***.

### B1-02
In Portuguese, turning an adjective into a noun (*um conhecido, os jovens, um alemão*) turns it into a normal noun with fixed `-o/-a/-s` endings. In German, it stays an adjective in disguise: *der Deutsch**e*** vs *ein Deutsch**er***. Also, *algo novo / nada de especial / tudo de bom* uses no preposition in German: *etwas Neues, nichts Besonderes, alles Gute*.

### B1-03
Portuguese distinguishes *pretérito perfeito* (*fui, fiz*) and *imperfeito* (*ia, fazia*) by aspect (one-time vs ongoing/habitual). German **Präteritum** and **Perfekt** have the **exact same meaning** and cover both *fui* and *ia*; the difference in German is purely **channel/register** (Perfekt in speech/e-mails, Präteritum in written articles and for auxiliaries/modals).

### B1-04
Portuguese uses *quando* for all three (*Quando eu era criança / Quando chove / Não sei quando ele vem*). Before writing "when" in German, pause and pick between **als** (1× past), **wenn** (repeated / present / future) and **wann** (question). The Plusquamperfekt matches PT *tinha feito* (*Quando cheguei, o trem já **tinha partido*** → *… **war** schon **abgefahren***).

### B1-05
In Brazilian Portuguese, *ir + infinitivo* (*vou viajar amanhã*) is the automatic choice for almost any future sentence. If you translate every PT *vou / vai* with *werde / wird*, you overuse Futur I. Rule for your B1 letter: if there is a time word (*am Montag, nächste Woche*), use **Präsens**; save *werden + Infinitiv* for promises (*Ich werde mich sofort darum kümmern*) and predictions (*Das wird sicher Spaß machen*).

### B1-06
The mapping to Portuguese is remarkably clean: Present: *Se eu **tivesse** (imperfeito do subjuntivo) tempo, **viajaria** (futuro do pretérito)* → *Wenn ich Zeit **hätte**, **würde** ich **reisen***. (Unlike PT where only the main clause takes *-ria*, German uses Konjunktiv II in **both** clauses!). · Past: *Se eu **tivesse sabido**, **teria vindo*** → *Wenn ich das **gewusst hätte**, **wäre** ich **gekommen***. · Past modal: *Você **deveria ter ligado*** → *Du **hättest** anrufen **sollen***.

### B1-07
Portuguese forms the passive with **ser / estar + particípio** (*A carta **é** escrita / **foi** escrita*). Because *ser* = *sein*, Brazilian learners automatically write ~~Der Brief ist geschrieben~~ when they mean an action! Lock in the contrast: PT **ser** + particípio (action) → German **werden** + Partizip II (*Der Brief **wird** / **wurde** geschrieben*). · PT **estar** + particípio (resulting state) → German **sein** + Partizip II (*Der Brief **ist** fertig geschrieben*). · PT *se*-passive (*Aluga-se apartamento / Fala-se alemão*) → German **man** or **wird + Partizip II**.

### B1-08
Portuguese uses *para / sem / em vez de + infinitivo* (*para comprar, sem dizer, em vez de reclamar*) directly after the preposition. German splits the two parts: **um / ohne / statt** opens the clause after the comma, all objects/adverbs sit in the middle, and **zu + Infinitiv** closes the clause at the end: *…, **um** ein neues Auto **zu kaufen***. Also, **lassen + Infinitiv** corresponds to both *deixar fazer* (*Lass mich schlafen*) and *mandar fazer* (*Ich lasse das Auto waschen*).

### B1-09
*embora + subjuntivo* (*embora esteja chovendo*) → **obwohl + Indikativ** (*obwohl es regnet* — normal present/past tense, no subjunctive!) · *desde que (tempo)* → **seit(dem)**; *assim que / logo que* → **sobald**; *até que* → **bis**; *caso (venha)* → **falls** (+ Indikativ) · *fazendo / ao fazer* (gerúndio de meio: *aprende lendo*) → German has no gerund for means; use **indem + subject + verb at end** (*…, indem er liest*).

### B1-10
*tanto … quanto / como* → **sowohl … als auch** · *não só … mas também* → **nicht nur …, sondern auch** · *ou … ou* → **entweder … oder** · *nem … nem* → **weder … noch** (Note: PT requires double negation with a verb before it — *Não tenho nem tempo nem vontade*; German forbids *nicht/kein* with *weder … noch*: *Ich habe weder Zeit noch Lust*) · *quanto mais …, (tanto) mais …* → **je mehr …, desto mehr …** (watch the V-END in *je* and V2 in *desto*!).

### B1-11
In Portuguese, *porque (denn/weil)*, *por isso (deshalb)*, *embora (obwohl)* and *mesmo assim (trotzdem)* all put the subject before the verb (*Por isso eu fico / Mesmo assim eu vou*). In German, the three columns have three completely different verb positions. Training your eye to check **where the verb sits** before picking the word is the single highest-yield exam tactic.

### B1-12
PT *a quem / para quem* → **dem / der / denen** (no preposition in German for pure Dativ!) · PT *em que / com quem / sobre o qual* → **in dem / mit dem / über den** (preposition + declined pronoun) · PT *cujo / cuja / cujos / cujas* agrees with the **possessed thing** (*o homem **cuja** filha*). German **dessen / deren** agrees with the **owner** before the comma (*der Mann [m] → **dessen** Tochter; die Frau [f] → **deren** Sohn*)! · PT *tudo o que / nada que* → *alles, **was** / nichts, **was***.

### B1-13
In Portuguese, you often drop the preposition before *que* or an infinitive (*Lembro que você veio / Fico feliz em ajudar*), or say *depende se …*. In German, the verb's preposition cannot simply vanish before a clause — it turns into **`da(r)-`** inside the main clause: *Ich erinnere mich **daran**, dass … / Es hängt **davon** ab, ob …*. Using two or three of these in your B1 letter immediately signals solid B1 grammar.

### B1-14
In Portuguese (and English), reported speech shifts the tense backwards when the reporting verb is in the past (*Ele disse que **estava** doente / que **tinha ido***). In German, **there is no automatic backshift of tense**! Even after *Er sagte* (past), German keeps the present tense of the original statement: *Er sagte, dass er krank **ist** (or **sei**)*. Do not turn present statements into *war / hatte* unless the event was already past when the speaker spoke.

### B1-15
Hearsay `soll`: PT uses *Dizem que o filme é ótimo* or *O filme parece ser ótimo*; German packs "they say" into one verb: *Der Film **soll** super **sein***. Don't translate *soll* here as *deve (obrigação)*! · Deduction: *Deve estar em casa* → *Er **muss** zu Hause sein*; *Não pode ser verdade!* → *Das **kann nicht** wahr sein!* · *Não precisa vir* → *Du **musst** nicht kommen* OR *Du **brauchst** nicht **zu** kommen* (never *darfst nicht*!).

### B1-16
`-ung` ≈ PT `-ção` (*die Information, die Reservierung = a reserva, die Erklärung = a explicação*) · `-heit / -keit` ≈ PT `-dade / -eza` (*die Möglichkeit = a possibilidade, die Freiheit = a liberdade*) · `-bar` ≈ PT `-vel` (*essbar = comestível, erreichbar = alcançável*) · `-los` ≈ PT *sem + substantivo* (*kostenlos = sem custo / gratuito*) · PT *ao ler / durante a leitura* → German **beim Lesen** (*bei dem* + capitalised infinitive!).

### B1-17
PT uses *o mesmo carro* for both one shared car and two identical cars; German separates **dasselbe Auto** (1 car) and **das gleiche Auto** (2 identical cars) · PT *um com o outro / uns aos outros* → one word in German: **miteinander / einander** · PT drops the dummy subject (*Trata-se de um erro / Acho importante que…*). German **requires `es`**: *Es handelt sich um einen Fehler / Ich finde **es** wichtig, dass …*.

### B1-18
1. **Double negation trap:** Portuguese requires double negation whenever *nada / ninguém / nunca* follows the verb (*Não vi ninguém / Não disse nada / Nunca fiz nada*). Translating the *não* gives ~~Ich habe nicht niemanden gesehen~~ — a major grammar error in German! Drop the *nicht*: *Ich habe **niemanden** gesehen.* 2. **`kein … mehr`:** PT *não tenho mais tempo* → German wraps *kein … mehr* around the noun: *Ich habe **keine** Zeit **mehr***. 3. **Two pronouns:** PT *Ele me disse isso* puts *me* (■) before *isso* (▲). German puts ▲ first: *Er hat **es mir** gesagt*.

### B1-19
Partizip I (`-d`) corresponds to PT `-nte` adjectives (*seguinte → folgend-, urgente → dringend-*) or a relative clause *que está fazendo* (*die schlafenden Kinder* = *as crianças que estão dormindo*). Never use Partizip I for a continuous tense after *sein* (~~Ich bin arbeitend~~ does NOT exist!) · Partizip II corresponds directly to PT past participles used as adjectives (*a conta paga* → *die bezahlte Rechnung*, *o contrato assinado* → *der unterschriebene Vertrag*), except in German the participle + all its modifiers stand **before** the noun!

### B1-20
PT *dentro de / no prazo de 14 dias* → **innerhalb von 14 Tagen** (or *innerhalb zwei**er** Wochen*) · PT *fora do horário de atendimento* → **außerhalb der Öffnungszeiten**; *exceto eu* → **außer mir** (■!) · PT *ao longo do rio* → **den Fluss entlang** (postposed!); *em frente ao banco* → **gegenüber der Bank** (no *de/von*!) · PT *no campo (interior)* → **auf dem Land**; *na praia* → **am Strand** (not *in*!).

### B1-21
In Portuguese e-mails, the first line after *Prezado Sr. Silva,* starts with a capital letter (*Venho por meio desta…*). In German, the salutation ends with a **comma**, so the first word of the body continues the sentence and is **lowercase** (*vielen Dank… / leider muss ich…*) — unless that first word is **Ich**, **Sie**, or a noun! · In Portuguese, there is a comma after *Atenciosamente,*. In German, **Mit freundlichen Grüßen** has **no comma and no full stop**!
