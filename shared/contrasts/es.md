# Language Pack — Spanish (`es`)

**Code:** `es` · **Language:** Spanish · **Flag:** 🇪🇸 · **Label:** Spanish contrast

Master reference for Spanish speakers (or learners who know Spanish as an L1/L2/L3) learning German up to telc B1. `python3 review/review.py lang sync` uses the `## Topic notes` section below to populate the `> 🇪🇸 **Spanish contrast**` boxes across `A1/`, `A2/`, and `B1/`.

## 1. Nouns and articles

| German feature | Spanish transfer | Contrast note & key traps |
|---|---|---|
| Three genders (`der / die / das`) | helps (2 arbitrary genders: *el / la*, plus neuter *lo*), but gender does not transfer | Learn every noun with `der/die/das`: *el sol* → **die** Sonne, *la luna* → **der** Mond, *la mesa* → **der** Tisch, *el coche* → **das** Auto. Notice `-problema / -tema / -clima` are masculine in Spanish (*el problema*) but **neuter (`das`)** in German (*das Problem, das Thema, das Klima*)! |
| Article agreement | helps (*el/la/los/las, un/una*) | German articles also inflect for **four cases** (`● ▲ ■ ◆`), not only gender and number. |
| Noun capitalisation | misleads | Every German noun is capitalised (*das Haus, das Lesen*). Days, months, and languages are capitalised in German (unlike Spanish *lunes, mayo, español*), while nationality adjectives are lowercase (*spanisch*). |
| Plural: many patterns + umlaut | neither (ES adds `-s / -es`) | German has 5 plural patterns (`-e, -er, -(e)n, -s, —` + umlaut). The `-s` plural (*Autos, Handys*) is only for loanwords. |
| Compound nouns | misleads (ES uses `N + de + N`: *mesa de comedor*) | German fuses compounds from right to left; the **last** word determines gender and plural (*das Esszimmer* ← *das Zimmer*). |
| Zero article & professions | helps! (*Soy médico / Hablo español*) | Like Spanish (*Es profesor*), German uses no article after `sein/werden` for professions and nationalities (*Er ist Lehrer*), and no article for indefinite plurals (*Tengo amigos → Ich habe Freunde*). However, unlike Spanish (*Me gusta **la** música / **Los** niños juegan*), German drops the definite article for general uncountables/plurals (*Ich mag **—** Musik*)! |

## 2. Cases (`● ▲ ■ ◆`)

| German feature | Spanish transfer | Contrast note & key traps |
|---|---|---|
| Four cases on determiners/adjectives/pronouns | helps via pronouns (*yo / me / mí / le*) | Anchor cases to Spanish pronouns: `●` = *yo/él*, `▲` = *lo/la*, `■` = *le/les (a él)*, `◆` = *de*. |
| Personal *a* in Spanish (*Veo **a** mi hermano*) vs German `▲` | misleads! | Spanish puts ***a*** before human direct objects (*Conozco **a** tu padre / Veo **al** profesor*). Never use a preposition or `■` Dativ in German for a direct object: *Ich kenne **deinen** Vater (`▲`) / Ich sehe **den** Lehrer (`▲`)*! |
| `■` Dativ verbs & *gustar*-type verbs | **strong anchor** for *gustar / encantar / doler / faltar / pertenecer*, trap for *ayudar / agradecer / seguir* | Spanish *gustar*-verbs have the exact same structure as German: the **thing is the subject `●`** and the **person is `■`** (*Me gusta el libro / Me gustan los libros → Das Buch gefällt **mir** / Die Bücher gefallen **mir***; same for *schmecken, gehören, fehlen, passen, wehtun*)! Watch the verbs that take a direct object in many Spanish varieties (*ayudar, agradecer, responder, seguir, felicitar*) — in German they always take **`■` Dativ** (*helfen, danken, antworten, folgen, gratulieren*). |
| Two-way prepositions (`wo? ■` vs `wohin? ▲`) | helps (*en* vs *a*: *estoy **en** el cine* vs *voy **al** cine*) | Spanish changes the preposition (*en* = location vs *a* = movement); German keeps the same preposition (`in, auf, an…`) and changes the **case**: `■` for location (*im Kino*), `▲` for movement (*ins Kino*). |
| `◆` Genitiv = possession | helps (*la casa **del** profesor*) | Written German uses `◆` (*das Haus **des** Lehrers*); spoken German uses `von + ■`. |

## 3. Verbs and tenses

| German feature | Spanish transfer | Contrast note & key traps |
|---|---|---|
| Obligatory subject pronoun (no pro-drop!) | **major trap!** (ES drops *yo, tú, él…* and dummy *llueve*) | Never drop the subject pronoun in German: *Vivo en Berlín → **Ich** wohne in Berlin*; *Llueve / Hace frío → **Es** regnet / **Es** ist kalt*. |
| One verb `sein` (vs *ser* and *estar*) | helps (simplification!) | German `sein` covers **both** *ser* and *estar* (*Soy profesor / Estoy cansado / Estoy en Madrid → Ich **bin** Lehrer / müde / in Madrid*). No continuous *estar + gerundio* (*Estoy comiendo → Ich esse gerade*). *Tener hambre / sed / miedo / razón / suerte* = *Hunger / Durst / Angst / Recht / Glück **haben***, **except age**: *Tengo 30 años → Ich **bin** 30 Jahre alt*! |
| `du / ihr / Sie` | **exact match** with Peninsular Spanish (*tú / vosotros / usted, ustedes*)! | `du` = *tú*; `ihr` = *vosotros / ustedes (informal plural)*; `Sie` (capitalised, verb in `-en`) = *usted* AND *ustedes (formal)*. |
| Perfekt (`haben/sein`) vs Präteritum | misleads (ES *indefinido / imperfecto / perfecto* split by aspect/recency) | German does **not** distinguish *comí* (indefinido) from *comía* (imperfecto) or *he comido* (perfecto) by meaning! Instead, German splits by **channel**: in speech & letters, use **Perfekt** for almost everything (*Gestern habe ich gegessen* = *ayer comí*) and **Präteritum** for `war, hatte, wurde, modals, wusste, es gab`; in written news/stories, use **Präteritum** throughout. |
| `sein` as Perfekt auxiliary | misleads (ES always uses *haber*: *he ido, ha venido*) | Spanish uses *haber* for every compound tense. In German, movement from A to B (*gehen, fahren, kommen, fliegen*), change of state (*aufstehen, einschlafen, werden*), and `sein / bleiben / passieren` take **`sein`** (*Ich **bin** nach Berlin gefahren*). |
| `seit` + Präsens | helps! (*Hace tres años que vivo aquí / Llevo tres años viviendo aquí*) | Like Spanish *hace … que + presente / llevar + gerundio*, German uses **`seit + ■` + Präsens** for an action that started in the past and continues now (*Ich **wohne** seit drei Jahren hier*). |
| Modal verbs | helps (*poder, deber, querer*) | Split Spanish *poder* into **können** (ability/possibility) and **dürfen** (permission). *No tienes que venir* = **nicht müssen / nicht brauchen zu**; *No debes / No puedes fumar* (prohibition) = **nicht dürfen**. |
| Konjunktiv II (`würde, hätte, wäre`) | **strong anchor!** (*condicional `-ría`* & *imperfecto de subjuntivo `-ra/-se`*) | *Si **tuviera** tiempo, **viajaría*** → *Wenn ich Zeit **hätte**, **würde** ich **reisen*** (Konjunktiv II in **both** clauses!). Past: *Si lo **hubiera sabido**, **habría venido*** → *Wenn ich das **gewusst hätte**, **wäre** ich **gekommen***. |
| Passiv (`werden` vs `sein`) | **strong anchor!** (*ser + participio* vs *estar + participio*) | Just as Spanish separates **ser + participio** (action: *La puerta **es / fue** abierta*) from **estar + participio** (resulting state: *La puerta **está** abierta*), German separates **`werden` + Partizip II** (action: ***wird / wurde** geöffnet*) from **`sein` + Partizip II** (state: ***ist** geöffnet*)! Spanish *se*-passive (*Se habla alemán*) = German **`man`** (*Man spricht Deutsch*). |

## 4. Word order and connectors

| German feature | Spanish transfer | Contrast note & key traps |
|---|---|---|
| V2 inversion (*Morgen fahre ich*) | misleads (*Mañana yo viajo*) | Whatever stands in Position 1 (time, place, object, subordinate clause), the conjugated verb **must be in Position 2**, pushing the subject behind it: *Morgen **fahre ich** nach Berlin*. |
| Verb-final in Nebensatz (`weil, dass, wenn, ob`) | misleads (*porque estoy cansado*) | Every Subjunktor pushes the conjugated verb to the **end** of the clause, with an obligatory comma before the conjunction (*Ich glaube**,** dass er morgen **kommt***). |
| Single negation (`niemand / nichts / nie`, `kein … mehr`) | **major trap!** (ES requires double negation: ***No** vi a **nadie***) | Spanish requires *no* before the verb when *nadie / nada / nunca* follows (*No dije nada*). German forbids double negation: *Ich habe **nichts** gesagt / Ich habe **niemanden** gesehen*! And *ya no tengo tiempo* wraps **`kein- … mehr`** around the noun: *Ich habe **keine** Zeit **mehr***. |
| Pronoun order (`es ihm`) | misleads (*Se lo doy / Me lo da*) | Spanish puts the indirect object pronoun (*me, te, le/se*) **before** the direct object (*lo, la*): ***Se lo** doy / **Me lo** dio*. German is the **exact opposite**: the `▲` direct pronoun (`ihn, es, sie`) always comes **before** the `■` indirect pronoun (`mir, dir, ihm, ihr, Ihnen`): *Ich gebe **es ihm** / Er gab **es mir***! |

## 5. Spelling, sounds, and false friends

- **Spelling & sounds:** Never write Spanish upside-down question/exclamation marks (`¿ ¡`) or acute accents (`á, é, í, ó, ú`) in German! German `v` is pronounced "f" (*Vater*), while `w` is pronounced "v" (*Wasser* — do not pronounce `w` like English/Spanish *gu/u*!). German `z` is sharp "ts" (*Zeit*). German `h` at the start of a word is aspirated (*Haus*), and after a vowel makes it long (*wohnen*). `ie` = long "ee" (*Liebe*); `ei` = "ai" (*mein* — like Spanish *aire*). · **Cognates & False friends ES ↔ DE:** · Latin cognates help in Lesen: *die Information, die Universität, die Situation, die Kultur, die Familie, die Natur, das Problem, das Konzert*. · False friends: *der Termin* = appointment (not *término/end*), *das Gymnasium* = academic high school (not gym = *das Fitnessstudio*), *bekommen* = receive (*recibir*, not become!), *der Chef* = boss (*el jefe*, not cook = *der Koch*), *aktuell* = current (*actual*), *eventuell* = possibly (*posiblemente*, not *eventualmente*), *sympathisch* = nice/likeable (*simpático*), *die Kaution* = deposit (*fianza*).

## Topic notes

### A1-01
Spanish never capitalises common nouns, days of the week (*el lunes → am **M**ontag*), months (*en mayo → im **M**ai*), or languages (*hablo español → ich spreche **S**panisch*), so check noun capitals last in every text. German has **no `¿ ¡` marks and no acute accents (`á, é, í, ó, ú`)** — only `ä, ö, ü` and `ß`. Pronounce `ei` like Spanish *ai* (*mein*), `ie` as long *i* (*Liebe*), `v` as "f" (*Vater*), `w` as "v" (*Wasser*), and `z` as "ts" (*Zeit*).

### A1-02
**Top trap for Spanish speakers:** Spanish is a pro-drop language (*Vivo en Madrid*), whereas in German **the subject pronoun is always obligatory** (*Ich wohne in Madrid*). Nor is there an *estar + gerundio* progressive (*Estoy estudiando → Ich lerne gerade*). For "you", map `du` to *tú*, `ihr` to *vosotros* (or informal Latin American *ustedes* for friends), and `Sie` (always capitalised, verb in `-en`) to formal *usted* AND *ustedes*.

### A1-03
German `sein` covers **both** Spanish *ser* and *estar* (*Soy médico / Estoy cansado / Estoy en casa → Ich **bin** Arzt / müde / zu Hause*). Many Spanish *tener* idioms use `haben` (*tener hambre / sed / miedo / razón / suerte → Hunger / Durst / Angst / Recht / Glück **haben***), **except age**, which uses `sein` (*Tengo 30 años → Ich **bin** 30 Jahre alt*!). Strong verbs change their stem vowel (`e→i/ie`, `a→ä`) only in `du` and `er/sie/es` (unlike Spanish *tengo / tienes / tiene / tienen*, where 1st person sg and 3rd person pl also change).

### A1-04
In Spanish, you can put a time or place at the start of a sentence without moving the subject (*Mañana yo trabajo*). In German, the conjugated verb is locked in **Position 2**: fronting *Morgen* forces the subject right behind the verb (*Morgen **arbeite ich***). Questions never use `¿`; just put the W-word + verb + subject (*¿Dónde vives? → **Wo wohnst du?***) or verb first (*¿Vienes? → **Kommst du?***).

### A1-05
Like Spanish (*el / la*), German grammatical gender is arbitrary, so learn every noun with `der / die / das` — never assume the Spanish gender transfers (*el sol → **die** Sonne, la luna → **der** Mond, la mesa → **der** Tisch*). Watch Greek-origin words in `-ma` (*el problema, el tema, el clima*): they are masculine in Spanish, but **neuter (`das Problem, das Thema, das Klima`)** in German! Like Spanish (*Soy profesor*), drop the article before professions after *sein* (*Ich bin Lehrer*).

### A1-06
In Spanish, almost every plural simply adds `-s` or `-es` (*casas, mujeres*). German has five plural patterns (`-e, -er, -(e)n, -s, —`) plus frequent umlauts (*das Haus → die Häuser, der Apfel → die Äpfel*). Feminine nouns (`die`) take `-(e)n` over 90 % of the time (*die Frau → die Frauen*), while the `-s` plural (*die Autos, die Fotos, die Sofas*) is reserved for foreign loanwords.

### A1-07
Spanish uses *no* before the verb for everything (*No trabajo / No tengo coche*). German splits negation in two: use **`kein-`** before a noun that would have *ein* or no article (*No tengo coche / tiempo → Ich habe **kein** Auto / **keine** Zeit*), and **`nicht`** everywhere else — placed **after** the conjugated verb (*Ich arbeite heute **nicht***). And **never use double negation**: *No vi a nadie → Ich habe **niemanden** gesehen* (no *nicht*!).

### A1-08
**Watch the Spanish "personal *a*" trap!** In Spanish, human direct objects take the preposition *a* (*Veo **a** mi hermano / Busco **al** médico*). In German, direct objects take the **`▲` Akkusativ with NO preposition** (*Ich sehe **meinen** Bruder / Ich suche **den** Arzt*). Only masculine singular changes its article in `▲` (`der → den, ein → einen, kein → keinen`).

### A1-09
In Spanish, *su / sus* can mean *his, her, its, their,* or *your (usted/ustedes)* (*su coche*). German separates them by the **owner** first: **sein-** (his/its), **ihr-** (her/their), and capitalised **Ihr-** (your — formal *usted/ustedes*), and then adds the ending for the **possessed noun** (*sein Auto, seine Schwester* vs *ihr Auto, ihre Schwester* vs *Ihr Auto*).

### A1-10
Spanish has no separable verbs (*levantarse, llamar, empezar* stay in one piece). In a German main clause, the stressed prefix splits off and goes to the **very end** of the sentence (*Te llamo mañana → Ich **rufe** dich morgen **an***), creating the *Satzklammer* (sentence bracket).

### A1-11
Spanish *poder, deber/tener que, querer + infinitivo* works just like German modal verbs, except the main infinitive travels to the **very end** of the clause (*Hoy tengo que comprar pan → Ich **muss** heute Brot **kaufen***). For polite requests (*quisiera*), use **ich möchte**. Unlike Spanish *Me gusta nadar*, `mögen` cannot take a verb infinitive — use **verb + gern** (*Ich schwimme gern*).

### A1-12
Like Spanish (*¡Ven! ¡Venid! ¡Venga usted!*), the German imperative puts the verb first, drops the pronoun for `du` (*Komm!*) and `ihr` (*Kommt!*), and keeps `Sie` right after the verb for formal requests (*Kommen Sie bitte!*). Remember that `e→i/ie` verbs keep their vowel change in `du` (*toma → **Nimm!**; lee → **Lies!***), without adding `-st`.

### A1-13
German numbers from 21 to 99 read the **units before the tens** (*veinticuatro* = 20+4 → *vierundzwanzig* = 4-and-20). Watch **`halb`** on the clock: Spanish *las ocho y media* (8:30) looks back at 8, whereas German **halb neun** (8:30 = "half towards nine") looks forward to the **next** hour! Time prepositions: *a las 8 → **um** 8 Uhr*; *el lunes → **am** Montag* (no article *el*, use `am`!); *en mayo / en verano → **im** Mai / **im** Sommer*; standalone years take **no preposition** (*en 2026 → **2026***).

### A1-14
Spanish uses *a* for any destination (*Voy a Berlín / al médico / a Suiza / a casa*) and *en* for location. German chooses the destination preposition by **what kind of place** it is: **nach** for cities, article-less countries, and home (*nach Berlin, nach Spanien, nach Hause*); **zu + `■`** for people, professions, and businesses (*zum Arzt, zu Paul, zur Arbeit*); and **in + `▲`** when entering a building or a country with an article (*ins Kino, in die Schweiz*).

### A1-15
**Your Spanish superpower for `gefallen / schmecken / gehören / fehlen / passen / wehtun`:** These verbs work **100 % like Spanish *gustar, encantar, pertenecer, faltar, quedar bien, doler***! The thing is the `●` subject and the person is `■` Dativ (*Me gusta el libro / Me gustan los libros → Das Buch gefällt **mir** / Die Bücher gefallen **mir***; *Me duele la cabeza → Der Kopf tut **mir** weh*). Also memorise the verbs where Spanish often uses a direct object or *a*, which always require **`■` Dativ** in German: *helfen (ayudar), danken (agradecer), antworten (responder), folgen (seguir), gratulieren (felicitar)*.

### A1-16
In Spanish, *pretérito indefinido* (*ayer comí*) is a single word for completed past actions, while *pretérito perfecto* (*he comido*) always uses the auxiliary *haber*. In German, **Perfekt** (*ich habe gegessen / ich bin gefahren*) is the standard past tense for **all** conversations, messages, and personal e-mails (*Ayer comí pizza → Gestern **habe** ich Pizza **gegessen***). And unlike Spanish *haber*, German switches the auxiliary to **`sein`** for movement from A to B (*gehen, fahren, kommen, fliegen*), change of state (*aufstehen, einschlafen*), and *sein / bleiben / passieren* (*Ayer fui a Colonia → Gestern **bin** ich nach Köln **gefahren***).

### A1-17
Spanish distinguishes *fui / era*, *tuve / tenía*, and *pude / podía* by aspect (one-time event vs ongoing background). German has **no aspect difference** between Präteritum and Perfekt! For `sein`, `haben`, and the modal verbs, simply use the **Präteritum** (*ich war, hatte, konnte, musste, wollte*) for **both** *fui/era*, *tuve/tenía*, and *pude/podía* in speech and writing alike. And Spanish *había / hubo* = **es gab**.

### A1-18
Unlike Spanish (*La casa es nueva / Las casas son nuevas*, where the adjective always agrees in gender and number), a German adjective after `sein / werden / bleiben` (**predicative**) has **no ending at all** (*Das Haus ist **neu** / Die Häuser sind **neu***). Adjectives also work as adverbs without adding *-mente* (*rápido / rápidamente → **schnell***). And Spanish *Me gusta cocinar / Me gusta más… / Lo que más me gusta…* = **verb + gern / lieber / am liebsten** (*Ich koche gern*).

### A1-19
Spanish coordinating conjunctions *y, o, pero* match German position-0 connectors (**und, oder, aber, denn** — verb stays second). However, adverbs like *luego, después, primero* (**dann, danach, zuerst**) take **Position 1** and force the verb ahead of the subject (*Luego voy a casa → Dann **gehe ich** nach Hause*). Inside the clause, Spanish often puts place before time (*Voy a Berlín mañana*); German puts **Time before Place** (*Ich fahre **morgen nach Berlin***).

### A1-20
In Spanish, impersonal sentences drop the subject (*Llueve, Hace frío, Hay un problema*) or use *se / uno* (*Aquí se habla alemán*). In German, you **must** write the pronoun: dummy **`es`** for weather, time, and existence (***Es** regnet, **Es** ist kalt, **Es** gibt + `▲`*), and **`man`** for Spanish *se / uno / la gente* (*Hier spricht **man** Deutsch*). For object pronouns referring to things, follow the **German** noun gender: *la mesa (f) → der Tisch (m) → **er***!

### A2-01
Like Spanish indirect objects (*Le doy el libro **al niño*** — note that German never doubles the noun with a redundant clitic *le*!), the person is `■` Dativ and the thing is `▲` Akkusativ (*Ich gebe **dem Kind** das Buch*). **Watch the two-pronoun reversal:** in Spanish (*Me lo da / Se lo doy*), the indirect object pronoun (*me, te, le/se*) comes **first** and the direct object (*lo, la*) comes **second**. In German, it is the **exact opposite** — the `▲` direct pronoun (`es, ihn, sie`) always comes **before** the `■` indirect pronoun (`mir, dir, ihm, ihr, Ihnen`): *Er gibt **es mir** / Ich gebe **es ihm***!

### A2-02
Spanish marks location vs destination with two different prepositions: ***en*** for location (*El libro está **en** la mesa / Estoy **en** el cine*) vs ***a*** for movement (*Pongo el libro **en/sobre** la mesa / Voy **al** cine*). German uses the **same** preposition (`auf, in, an, über, unter, vor, hinter, neben, zwischen`) for both and switches the **case**: **wo?** (location) → **`■` Dativ** (*auf **dem** Tisch, **im** Kino*); **wohin?** (destination) → **`▲` Akkusativ** (*auf **den** Tisch, **ins** Kino*).

### A2-03
Spanish ***para*** and ***por*** split by meaning in German: *para* (recipient / benefit / duration: *para ti, para tres días*) → **für + `▲`** (*für dich, für drei Tage*) · *para / a* (destination: *voy para Berlín / al médico*) → **nach / zu + `■`** (*nach Berlin, zum Arzt*) · *por* (through space / cause: *por el parque*) → **durch + `▲`** (*durch den Park*) · *sin* → **ohne + `▲`**; *con* → **mit + `■`**; *desde (origen)* → **aus / von + `■`**; *desde hace (tiempo)* → **seit + `■`**.

### A2-04
Compare Spanish time expressions with German: *Hace tres años que vivo aquí / Llevo tres años viviendo aquí* (still true!) → **`seit + ■` + Präsens** (*Ich **wohne** seit drei Jahren hier*) · *Vine hace tres años* (finished in the past, "ago") → **`vor + ■` + Perfekt/Präteritum** (*Ich bin **vor** drei Jahren gekommen*) · *Dentro de una hora* (in an hour from now) → **`in + ■`** (*in einer Stunde*) · *Solo* splits in two: **nur** for quantity (*solo dos → **nur** zwei*) vs **erst** for time "not until / only so far" (*solo llega a las 10 → er kommt **erst** um 10 Uhr*).

### A2-05
Because Spanish forms every compound past tense with *haber* (*he viajado, me he mudado, ha pasado, me he quedado*), your instinct will be to use `haben` for everything. Train the **`sein` reflex**: whenever there is **intransitive movement from A to B** (*gehen, fahren, fliegen, kommen, umziehen*) or a **change of state** (*aufwachen, einschlafen, wachsen, sterben, werden*) or **`sein / bleiben / passieren`**, use **`sein`** (*Me he mudado → Ich **bin** umgezogen*). Conversely, all **reflexive verbs** take **`haben`** (*Me he cambiado de ropa → Ich **habe** mich umgezogen*).

### A2-06
When you read German news articles or stories (Leseverstehen!), you will see the **Präteritum** (*er ging, sie kam, wir sahen, er dachte*). Do not try to map Präteritum to Spanish *indefinido* (*fue*) and Perfekt to *pretérito perfecto* (*ha ido*) — in German they have the **exact same meaning** and only differ by text type (Präteritum in written narratives and for `war/hatte/konnte/wusste/es gab`; Perfekt in speech and letters).

### A2-07
Two high-frequency Spanish → German modal distinctions:
1. Spanish ***poder*** covers both ability (*Puedo nadar*) and permission (*¿Puedo entrar?*). German separates them: **können** (ability/possibility) vs **dürfen** (permission: ***Darf** ich reinkommen?*).
2. Spanish ***no deber / no poder*** (*No debes fumar aquí*) = **nicht dürfen** (prohibition: *Sie **dürfen** hier **nicht** rauchen*). German **nicht müssen** corresponds to Spanish ***no tener que / no hace falta*** (*No tienes que venir → Du **musst nicht** kommen*).

### A2-08
In Spanish, descriptive adjectives usually follow the noun (*el coche nuevo, la casa grande*) and their ending (`-o/-a/-os/-as`) only depends on gender and number. In German, attributive adjectives **always precede the noun** and their ending depends on the **determiner** before them and the **case**: after `der / die / das / dieser / jeder / welcher / alle` (Rule A), the ending is **`-e`** in only 5 cells (`●` m/n/f, `▲` n/f) and **`-en`** in every other cell.

### A2-09
After `ein / kein / mein` (Rule B), because *ein* has **no ending** in three cells (`●` m *ein*, `●` n *ein*, `▲` n *ein*), the adjective must carry the gender signal itself: *un hombre viejo → ein alt**er** Mann*, *un niño pequeño → ein klein**es** Kind*. Notice also the letter closings: *Con saludos cordiales → Mit freundlich**en** Grüß**en*** (`■` pl. after *mit*) vs *Saludos → Lieb**e** Grüß**e*** (`●/▲` pl. with no article).

### A2-10
In Spanish, almost all comparatives and superlatives are built with ***más*** (*más rápido, más interesante, el más grande*), with only 4 one-word exceptions (*mejor, peor, mayor, menor*). In German, **every** adjective — short or long! — forms its comparative with **`-er`** and superlative with **`am -sten / der -ste`** (*schneller, interessanter* — never ~~mehr interessant~~!). For "than" vs "as": Spanish *más … **que*** = **Komparativ + als** (*größer **als***); Spanish *tan … **como*** = **(genau)so … wie** (*so groß **wie***).

### A2-11
In Spanish, *que, porque, cuando, si* leave the verb right after the subject (*Creo que **viene** mañana / porque **estoy** enfermo*). In German, `dass, weil, wenn` send the conjugated verb to the **very end** of the clause, and the **comma before the conjunction is mandatory** (*Ich glaube**,** dass er morgen **kommt***). Also remember: never drop the subject pronoun inside the subordinate clause (*porque estoy cansado → weil **ich** müde **bin***)!

### A2-12
Spanish uses ***si*** for both conditions (*Si tengo tiempo*) and indirect yes/no questions (*No sé **si** viene*). German strictly separates them: condition = **wenn / falls**; indirect yes/no question ("whether") = **ob** (*Ich weiß nicht, **ob** er kommt*). For purpose clauses: Spanish *para + infinitivo* (same subject) = **um … zu + Infinitiv**, while Spanish *para que + subjuntivo* (different subjects: *para que entiendas*) = **damit + normal Indikativ at the end** (*damit du **verstehst*** — no subjunctive!).

### A2-13
Spanish *condicional* (`-ría`: *tendría, sería, compraría, podría, debería*) maps directly to German **Konjunktiv II**: *tendría → **hätte***, *sería → **wäre***, *podría → **könnte***, *debería → **sollte***, *querría / me gustaría → **hätte gern / würde gern …***, and for all other verbs **würde + Infinitiv** (*compraría → **würde … kaufen***).

### A2-14
Spanish is rich in reflexive verbs (*darse prisa → sich beeilen, alegrarse → sich freuen, acordarse de → sich erinnern an, quejarse de → sich beschweren über*), though some differ (*olvidarse → vergessen* [not reflexive], *casarse → heiraten* [not reflexive], *relajarse → sich erholen*). Remember that with a body part or piece of clothing (`▲`), the German reflexive pronoun is **`■` Dativ** (*Me lavo las manos → Ich wasche **mir** die Hände*). Memorise verb + preposition chunks as one unit: *esperar el autobús (no prep in ES!) → **warten auf ▲***, *pensar en → **denken an ▲***, *interesarse por → **sich interessieren für ▲***, *tener miedo de → **Angst haben vor ■***, *soñar con → **träumen von ■***.

### A2-15
In Spanish, one invariable word — ***que*** — introduces almost every relative clause (*el hombre **que** vive aquí / el hombre **que** vi ayer*). In German, you must pick the relative pronoun in **two steps**: (1) take **gender and number** from the noun before the comma (*der Mann* = masculine singular), and (2) take **case** from its role inside the relative clause (`●` subject → ***der** hier wohnt*; `▲` direct object → ***den** ich gestern gesehen habe*), and put the verb at the end!

### A2-16
In Spanish, connectors like *por eso, sin embargo, a pesar de eso, luego, además, de lo contrario* are followed by `Subject + Verb` (*Por eso yo me quedo en casa*). In German, `deshalb, trotzdem, dann, außerdem, sonst` are adverbs in **Position 1**, so the conjugated verb **must follow immediately in Position 2** before the subject: *Deshalb **bleibe ich** zu Hause / Trotzdem **gehen wir** spazieren*.

### A2-17
Where Spanish says *el mío / la mía* (*Este coche es el mío*) or *uno / ninguno*, German uses standalone pronouns carrying strong `der`-endings in the three bare cells: `●` m **meiner / einer / keiner**, `●/▲` n **meins / eins / keins**, `●/▲` f/pl **meine / eine / keine** (*¿Tienes un bolígrafo? — Sí, tengo uno → Ja, ich habe **einen*** [m!]). For indefinite plural "some/any" (*¿Tienes manzanas? — Sí, tengo algunas*), use **welche** (*Ja, ich habe **welche***).

### A2-18
Watch two contrasts between Spanish and German articles/quantifiers:
1. For **general plural or uncountable nouns** (*Me gustan **los** perros / **El** café es caro*), Spanish requires the definite article *el/la/los/las*, whereas German uses **zero article** (*Ich mag **—** Hunde / **—** Kaffee ist teuer*).
2. Spanish *mucho / poco* agrees everywhere (*mucha agua, muchos amigos*). In German, before uncountable singular nouns `viel / wenig` takes **no ending** (*viel Wasser, viel Zeit, wenig Geld*), and only takes plural endings before countable plurals (*viele Freunde, wenige Tage*).

### A2-19
`werden` translates three completely different Spanish structures depending on what follows it:
1. **`werden` + adjective/noun** = *ponerse / hacerse / volverse / llegar a ser* (*Se hizo tarde → Es **wurde** spät; Se hizo médico → Er **wurde** Arzt*).
2. **`werden` + Infinitiv** = future (*ir a + Inf.* / *futuro simple*). Note: in Spanish, *Voy a viajar mañana* is the default for plans; in German, whenever there is a time word (*morgen, nächste Woche*), **Präsens** is much more natural (*Morgen **reise** ich*)!
3. **`werden` + Partizip II** = passive (*ser + participio*: *La casa fue construida → Das Haus **wurde** gebaut*).

### A2-20
**Reversed double-pronoun order:** In Spanish, you always put the indirect object clitic (*me, te, le/se, nos*) before the direct object clitic (*lo, la, los, las*): ***Me lo** explicó / **Se la** regalé*. In German, whenever both objects are pronouns, the **`▲` direct pronoun (`ihn, es, sie`) comes FIRST** and the **`■` indirect pronoun (`mir, dir, ihm, ihr, uns, Ihnen`) comes SECOND**: *Er hat **es mir** erklärt / Ich habe **sie ihm** geschenkt*!

### A2-21
In Spanish, you use *preposición + pronombre / qué / quién* for both things and people (*¿En qué piensas? — Pienso en eso / ¿En quién piensas? — Pienso en ella*). German splits **things** from **people**: Also note key Spanish → German preposition mismatches: *agradecer por → sich bedanken **für ▲***, *pedir algo (no prep in ES!) → bitten **um ▲***, *participar en → teilnehmen **an ■***, *depender de → abhängen **von ■***. **Thing:** fuse **`wo(r)-`** (question) and **`da(r)-`** (pronoun): ***Woran** denkst du? — Ich denke **daran*** (never ~~an es~~!) · **Person:** keep **preposition + `wen / wem` or personal pronoun**: ***An wen** denkst du? — Ich denke **an sie***.

### B1-01
Spanish ***de / del / de la / de los / de las*** maps directly to the German `◆ Genitiv`: *la oficina **del** jefe → das Büro **des** Chefs*; *a causa **de la** lluvia → wegen **des** Regens*; *a pesar **del** frío → trotz **der** Kälte*; *durante **el** curso → während **des** Kurses*; *en vez **de un** regalo → statt **eines** Geschenks*. Don't forget to add **`-(e)s`** to masculine and neuter singular nouns too (*des Chef**s**, des Kind**es***).

### B1-02
In Spanish, an adjective can become a noun simply by putting *el / la / un / una / lo* in front of it (*un conocido, el alemán, los jóvenes, lo bueno*). In German, nominalised adjectives are **capitalised**, but they **still take adjective endings** according to Rules A, B, and C: *der Bekannt**e*** (after *der*) vs *ein Bekannt**er*** (after *ein*, `●` m!) vs *viele Bekannt**e*** (no article). And Spanish *algo nuevo / nada especial / todo lo mejor* = **etwas Neues / nichts Besonderes / alles Gute**.

### B1-03
In Spanish, learning past tenses means mastering the aspect contrast between *pretérito indefinido* (*ayer llovió / fui*) and *pretérito imperfecto* (*llovía / iba*). German does **not** have an imperfecto vs indefinido contrast! Both **Präteritum** (*es regnete / ich ging*) and **Perfekt** (*es hat geregnet / ich bin gegangen*) translate *llovió* AND *llovía*; Präteritum is simply the tense used in newspaper articles and narratives (plus `war, hatte, wurde`, modals, `wusste, es gab` everywhere).

### B1-04
Spanish uses ***cuando*** (or *cuándo*) for three distinct German words:
1. **als** = a single event or life stage in the past (*Cuando tenía 10 años / Cuando llegué ayer → **Als** ich 10 war / **Als** ich gestern ankam*).
2. **wenn** = repeated past (*Siempre que venía → Immer **wenn** er kam*) or any present/future event (*Cuando llegues, llámame → **Wenn** du ankommst, ruf mich an!* — with **Präsens**, never subjunctive!).
3. **wann** = direct or indirect questions (*¿Cuándo viene? / No sé cuándo viene → …, **wann** er kommt*).
The Plusquamperfekt matches Spanish *pluscuamperfecto* (*había comido / había salido → **hatte** gegessen / **war** abgefahren*), mandatory after **nachdem** (*después de que*).

### B1-05
Two key Spanish ↔ German matches in Futur I:
1. For scheduled personal plans (*Mañana **voy a viajar** a Múnich*), Spanish prefers *ir a + infinitivo*; German prefers **Präsens + time word** (*Morgen **fahre** ich nach München*).
2. Just as Spanish uses the *futuro simple* for **conjecture about the present** (*¿Dónde está Pedro? — **Estará** en un atasco* = "He's probably in a traffic jam right now"), German uses **`werden + (wohl) + Infinitiv`**: *Er **wird wohl** im Stau **stehen***!

### B1-06
Spanish and German have a near-perfect structural parallel for unreal conditionals: **Present unreal:** Spanish *Si **tuviera** (`imperfecto de subjuntivo`) más tiempo, **aprendería** (`condicional`) otro idioma* → German uses **Konjunktiv II in BOTH clauses**: *Wenn ich mehr Zeit **hätte**, **würde** ich noch eine Sprache **lernen*** · **Past unreal:** Spanish *Si me **hubieras avisado**, te **habría ayudado*** → German uses **`hätte / wäre + Partizip II` in BOTH clauses**: *Wenn du mir Bescheid **gesagt hättest**, **hätte** ich dir **geholfen*** (*Si lo hubiera sabido, habría venido → Wenn ich das **gewusst hätte**, **wäre** ich **gekommen***) · **Past modal advice:** *¡Deberías haberme llamado! → Du **hättest** mich anrufen **sollen**!*.

### B1-07
**Your best Spanish anchor for the Passive:** Spanish clearly separates **ser + participio** (process/action: *La puerta **es / fue** cerrada a las 8*) from **estar + participio** (resulting state: *La puerta **está / estaba** cerrada*). Map them 1:1 to German: Spanish **ser + participio** (action) = German **`werden` + Partizip II** (*Die Tür **wird / wurde** um 8 Uhr geschlossen; **ist** geschlossen **worden***) · Spanish **estar + participio** (state) = German **`sein` + Partizip II** (*Die Tür **ist / war** geschlossen*) · Spanish *pasiva refleja* with ***se*** (*Aquí no **se** fuma / **Se** reparan coches*) = German **`man`** or **`wird + Partizip II`** (*Hier raucht **man** nicht / Hier **wird** nicht geraucht*).

### B1-08
Spanish *para / sin / en vez de + infinitivo* (*para ahorrar, sin decir nada, en vez de quejarse*) maps directly to **`um … zu / ohne … zu / (an)statt … zu + Infinitiv`** whenever both clauses have the **same subject**! Place `um / ohne / statt` right after the comma, and `zu + Infinitiv` at the very end (*…, **um** Geld **zu sparen***). Notice also that Spanish *No tienes que / No hace falta venir* can be expressed with **`nicht brauchen + zu + Infinitiv`** (*Du **brauchst** nicht **zu kommen***).

### B1-09
Two big wins for Spanish speakers:
1. In Spanish, *aunque (hipotético), hasta que (futuro), en cuanto / tan pronto como, en caso de que* require the **subjuntivo** (*Llámame en cuanto **llegues** / En caso de que **tenga** preguntas*). In German, `obwohl, bis, sobald, falls` **always take the normal Indikativ** (with the verb at the end: *Ruf mich an, sobald du **ankommst** / Falls Sie Fragen **haben***)!
2. Spanish **gerundio of manner/means** (*Se ahorra energía **apagando** la luz*) has no gerund equivalent in German; always translate it with **`indem + subject + verb at end`** (*Man spart Energie, **indem man** das Licht **ausschaltet***).

### B1-10
Map the six two-part connectors to Spanish: *tanto … como* → **sowohl … als auch** · *no solo …, sino también* → **nicht nur …, sondern auch** · *o (bien) … o (bien)* → **entweder … oder** · *ni … ni* → **weder … noch** (Trap: in Spanish, when *ni … ni* follows the verb, you must put ***no*** before the verb: ***No** tengo **ni** tiempo **ni** ganas*. In German, **never add `nicht` or `kein`** with `weder … noch`: *Ich habe **weder** Zeit **noch** Lust*!) · *si bien / es cierto que …, (pero)* → **zwar …, aber** · *cuanto más …, (tanto) más …* (*Cuanto más practico, mejor hablo*) → **je + comparative (+ V-END!), desto + comparative (+ V2!)** (*Je mehr ich **übe**, desto besser **spreche ich***).

### B1-11
In Spanish, *porque (denn/weil)*, *por eso (deshalb)*, *aunque (obwohl)*, and *sin embargo / a pesar de eso (trotzdem)* all allow `Subject + Verb` right after them. In German, choosing the right connector in telc Sprachbausteine is a **verb-position check first**: verb at the end → Column 2 Subjunktor (`weil, da, obwohl, sodass, damit`); verb right after the gap → Column 3 Adverb (`deshalb, trotzdem, sonst`); subject + verb after the gap → Column 1 Konjunktor (`denn, aber, sondern`). Also notice that Spanish ***sino*** (after negation: *no hoy, **sino** mañana*) is the exact match of German **`sondern`** (*nicht heute, **sondern** morgen*)!

### B1-12
**Watch the Spanish *cuyo / cuya / cuyos / cuyas* trap!** In Spanish, *cuyo* agrees in gender and number with the **possessed noun after it** (*el hombre **cuya** hija… / la mujer **cuyo** hijo…*). In German, **`dessen` / `deren`** agrees with the **owner before the comma**: masculine or neuter owner → **`dessen`** (*der Mann [m], **dessen** Tochter…*); feminine or plural owner → **`deren`** (*die Frau [f], **deren** Sohn…*)! And Spanish *lo que / todo lo que* (*Todo **lo que** dices*) = German **`was`** (*Alles, **was** du sagst*).

### B1-13
In Spanish, before a *que*-clause or infinitive, prepositions either stay (*Me alegro **de que** vengas / Depende **de si** llueve*) or drop. In German, prepositions like *auf, für, über, um, an, von* cannot stand directly before *dass, ob,* or *zu + Infinitiv* — instead, you put the matching **`da(r)-` compound** (**darauf, dafür, darüber, darum, daran, davon**) in the main clause as a bridge pointing to the comma: *Ich freue mich **darauf**, **dass** du kommst / Es hängt **davon** ab, **ob** es regnet*.

### B1-14
In Spanish, reporting a statement after a past verb forces a tense backshift (*Dijo que **estaba** enfermo / que **vendría** / que ya **había comido***). In German, **there is no past backshift**! Even after *Er sagte* (past), keep the tense of the original statement: *Er sagte, dass er krank **ist** (or news Konjunktiv I: **sei**) / dass er morgen **kommt** (or **komme**)*. When reading news texts, recognise `er sei`, `er habe`, `es gebe`, and plural `sie hätten / würden` as reported speech ("according to…").

### B1-15
In Spanish, you say *Dicen que el nuevo hotel es muy bueno / Se dice que…*. German packs "they say / reportedly" into the modal verb **`sollen` in the Präsens**: *Das neue Hotel **soll** sehr gut **sein***. For probability deductions where Spanish uses the future (*Tendrá hambre / Estará en casa*), German uses **`muss / müsste / dürfte + Infinitiv`** (*Er **muss** zu Hause **sein***) or `wird wohl … sein`.

### B1-16
Many suffixes map directly from Spanish: Spanish `-ción / -miento` ≈ German **`-ung`** (always `die`: *die Reservierung, die Erklärung*); Spanish `-dad / -eza` ≈ German **`-heit / -keit`** (always `die`: *die Möglichkeit = la posibilidad, die Sicherheit = la seguridad*); Spanish `-ble` = German **`-bar`** (*lösbar, erreichbar*); Spanish *sin + noun* = **`-los`** (*kostenlos = gratuito / sin costo*). And Spanish *al leer / leyendo* = **`beim Lesen`** (`bei dem` + capitalised infinitive!).

### B1-17
Spanish uses *el mismo / la misma* for both one shared item and two identical items. German separates **derselbe / dasselbe / dieselbe** (one single shared object: *Vivimos en el mismo edificio → im **selben** Haus*) from **der / das / die gleiche** (an identical copy/model: *Tienes la misma chaqueta que yo → **die gleiche** Jacke*). Spanish *el uno con el otro / entre sí* fuses into **miteinander** (*füreinander, voneinander*). And Spanish *Se trata de…* requires the subject **`es`** in German: **Es handelt sich um + `▲`** / **Es geht um + `▲`** (*In dem Text **geht es um**…*).

### B1-18
Three essential Spanish → German negation rules:
1. **Never use double negation:** *No vi a nadie / No dije nada / Nunca voy* → *Ich habe **niemanden** gesehen / Ich habe **nichts** gesagt / Ich gehe **nie*** (never add *nicht*!).
2. ***todavía no* vs *ya no*:** *todavía no* = **noch nicht** (with a noun: *todavía no tengo respuesta → ich habe **noch keine** Antwort*); *ya no* = **nicht mehr** (with a noun: *ya no tengo tiempo → ich habe **keine** Zeit **mehr*** — wrap `kein- … mehr` around the noun!).
3. **Two pronouns (*Se lo envié*):** Always put `▲` (`ihn, es, sie`) **before** `■` (`mir, dir, ihm, ihr, Ihnen`): *Ich habe **ihn/es ihm** geschickt*.

### B1-19
Spanish *participio pasado* used as an adjective (*el contrato firmado, la mercancía entregada*) corresponds to German **Partizip II + adjective ending** (*der unterschrieben**e** Vertrag, die geliefert**e** Ware*), while active ongoing descriptions (*los niños que ríen / el próximo lunes / la semana siguiente*) use **Partizip I** (`Infinitiv + -d + ending`: *die lachend**en** Kinder, am kommend**en** Montag, in der folgend**en** Woche*). In B1 reading texts, remember that German puts the entire modifier **before** the noun (*el correo enviado ayer por usted → der **gestern von Ihnen geschickte** Brief*).

### B1-20
Compare high-frequency B1 prepositions with Spanish: *en un plazo de 14 días / dentro de una semana* → **innerhalb von 14 Tagen / innerhalb einer Woche (`◆`)**; *fuera del horario de apertura* → **außerhalb der Öffnungszeiten (`◆`)** · *excepto yo / salvo mi hermano* → **außer mir / außer meinem Bruder (`■`)** · *enfrente de la estación* → **gegenüber dem Bahnhof (`■`)** · *a lo largo del río* → **den Fluss entlang** (`▲`, *entlang* stands **after** the noun!) · *en mi opinión / según mi opinión* → **meiner Meinung nach** (*nach* stands **after** *Meinung*!) · *en el campo / al campo* → **auf dem Land / aufs Land**; *en la playa / a la playa* → **am Strand / an den Strand**.

### B1-21
Three key letter-format differences between Spanish and German:
1. **Punctuation after the salutation:** In Spanish, you use a colon (*Estimada señora Weber:**`***) and start the next line with a **capital** letter. In German, you use a **comma** (*Sehr geehrte Frau Weber**,***) and start the first word of the body in **lowercase** (*vielen Dank… / leider muss ich…*) unless it is a noun or `Sie`!
2. **Closing formula:** Unlike Spanish *Atentamente**,*** (with a comma), German **Mit freundlichen Grüßen** has **no comma and no full stop**.
3. **Capital `Sie / Ihnen / Ihr`:** Formal pronouns and possessives (*usted / ustedes / su*) are always capitalised in German.
