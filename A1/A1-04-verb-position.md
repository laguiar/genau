# A1-04 — Verb position: statements, W-questions, yes/no questions

**Level:** A1 · **Time:** 1–2 sessions · **Prerequisites:** A1-02 · **Nicos Weg:** [Woher kommst du?](https://learngerman.dw.com/de/woher-kommst-du/l-40368715), [Was machst du hier?](https://learngerman.dw.com/de/was-machst-du-hier/l-40378734)
**Can-do:** I can build a statement with the verb in second position (whatever comes first), a W-question, and a yes/no question with the verb first.

## 0. Diagnostic (3 min)

Build the sentence from the pieces, starting with the underlined part. ≥ 7/8 → `✔`.

1. _Heute_ / ich / arbeiten → …
2. _wo_ / du / wohnen → …?
3. yes/no question: du / kommen / aus Brasilien → …?
4. _Wir_ / morgen / Fußball / spielen → …
5. _was_ / ihr / machen → …?
6. yes/no question: Sie / sprechen / Deutsch → …?
7. _Am Montag_ / ich / Deutsch / lernen → …
8. _wer_ / das / sein → …?

## 1. The rule in one picture

```
 STATEMENT        [ Position 1 ]   [ VERB ]   [ the rest ]
                    Ich              wohne      in Berlin.
                    Heute            arbeite    ich.            ← subject jumps behind the verb
                    In Berlin        wohne      ich.
                    Meine Schwester  kommt      morgen.         (one element = one "slot", even if 2 words)

 W-QUESTION       [ W-word ]        [ VERB ]   [ the rest ]
                    Wo               wohnst     du?
                    Woher            kommen     Sie?
                    Wie viele Kinder hast       du?             (W-word + noun = one slot)

 YES/NO QUESTION  [ VERB ]          [ subject ] [ the rest ]
                    Wohnst           du         in Berlin?
                    Kommen           Sie        aus Brasilien?
```

**The verb is always in slot 2 in a statement and a W-question, and in slot 1 in a yes/no question.** That is the whole rule.

## 2. The rule in words

**Slot 1 is free, slot 2 is the verb.** You can start a statement with the subject, a time, a place, an object, or anything you want to emphasise. Whatever you start with, the conjugated verb comes **immediately after it**, and if the subject was not first, it comes **right after the verb**. *Ich lerne heute Deutsch* = *Heute lerne ich Deutsch* = *Deutsch lerne ich heute*. Same meaning, different focus.

**One slot can hold several words** if they form one unit: *Meine kleine Schwester* (one subject), *Am nächsten Montag* (one time), *In der Stadt* (one place).

**W-questions** put the question word in slot 1; the verb stays in slot 2. The W-words:

| | | |
|--|--|--|
| **wer** who | **was** what | **wo** where |
| **woher** where from | **wohin** where to | **wann** when |
| **wie** how | **warum** why | **wie viel** how much |
| **wie viele** how many | **wie lange** how long | **wie oft** how often |
| **welcher/welche/welches** which | **was für ein** what kind of | **wie alt / wie spät** how old / what time |

**Yes/no questions** put the verb in slot 1 and the subject right after it. There is **no helper verb** like English *do*: *Kommst du?* = *Do you come? / Are you coming?* Intonation rises at the end. The answer is *ja / nein*; after a negative question, *doch* means "yes, actually" (A1-07).

> 🇧🇷 **Portuguese contrast** — Portuguese keeps the verb after the subject even when something else comes first: *Hoje eu trabalho.* German must flip: *Heute **arbeite ich**.* And PT yes/no questions look like statements (*Você mora em Berlim?*); German moves the verb to the front: ***Wohnst** du in Berlin?* Question words are close to PT (*onde* = wo, *de onde* = woher, *para onde* = wohin, *quando* = wann, *quem* = wer, *o que* = was, *como* = wie, *por que* = warum).
> 🇪🇸 **Spanish contrast** — In Spanish, you can put a time or place at the start of a sentence without moving the subject (*Mañana yo trabajo*). In German, the conjugated verb is locked in **Position 2**: fronting *Morgen* forces the subject right behind the verb (*Morgen **arbeite ich***). Questions never use `¿`; just put the W-word + verb + subject (*¿Dónde vives? → **Wo wohnst du?***) or verb first (*¿Vienes? → **Kommst du?***).
> 🇫🇷 **French contrast** — In French, you can put an adverb in front and keep `Subject + Verb` (*Demain, je travaille*). In German, the conjugated verb is locked in **Position 2**, so the subject must move behind the verb: *Morgen **arbeite ich***. For yes/no questions, simply put the verb first (*Habites-tu à Berlin ?* → ***Wohnst** du in Berlin?* — no *est-ce que*!).
> 🇬🇧 **English contrast** — No *do/does*. *Do you live here?* → *Wohnst du hier?* And no *-ing*: *Where are you going?* → *Wohin gehst du?*

## 3. Watch out

- **Two things before the verb** is the classic error: ~~Heute ich arbeite~~ → *Heute arbeite ich.*
- *wo* means **where** (not "who"); *wer* means **who**. *wer/was* are also the ● forms (A1-08 for *wen/was*).
- *wo* (location) vs *wohin* (direction) vs *woher* (origin): *Wo wohnst du? — In Köln. Wohin fährst du? — Nach Köln. Woher kommst du? — Aus Köln.*
- *wie* asks manner and name: *Wie heißt du? Wie geht es dir? Wie alt bist du?*
- In a W-question the subject follows the verb: *Wann kommt **der Zug**?*

## 4. Exercises (no peeking)

**4.1 Recognise** — Mark slot 1, the verb, and the subject in each sentence:
1. Morgen fahre ich nach Hamburg.
2. Meine Eltern wohnen in Porto Alegre.
3. Am Wochenende spielen wir Tennis.
4. Wann beginnt der Kurs?
5. Trinkst du Kaffee?

**4.2 Reorder** — Start with the underlined element:
1. ich / _jetzt_ / Deutsch / lernen
2. _mein Bruder_ / in Brasilien / wohnen
3. _am Abend_ / wir / Pizza / essen
4. _die Kinder_ / heute / nicht / spielen
5. _in Berlin_ / man / viel Bier / trinken
6. _um acht Uhr_ / der Film / beginnen

**4.3 Make the question** — Write the question that the underlined part answers:
1. Ich wohne _in München_.
2. Er heißt _Paul_.
3. Wir kommen _aus Brasilien_.
4. Sie arbeitet _bei Bosch_.
5. Der Kurs beginnt _um neun Uhr_.
6. Ich bin _32 Jahre alt_.
7. Sie fahren _nach Wien_.
8. Ich lerne Deutsch, _weil ich in Deutschland arbeite_.

**4.4 Yes/no questions** — Turn into a question:
1. Du kommst aus Portugal.
2. Sie sprechen Englisch. (formal)
3. Ihr wohnt in Köln.
4. Das Kind schläft.
5. Er hat Zeit.

**4.5 Produce** — Write: two statements starting with a time expression, two W-questions with *wohin/woher*, one yes/no question to a stranger (*Sie*).
