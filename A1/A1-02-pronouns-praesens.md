## 1. The rule in one picture

```
            stem = infinitive minus -en        wohn|en   arbeit|en   heiß|en
            ┌────────┬────────┬──────────┬──────────┐
            │        │ wohnen │ arbeiten │ heißen   │
            ├────────┼────────┼──────────┼──────────┤
 ich        │  -e    │ wohne  │ arbeite  │ heiße    │
 du         │  -st   │ wohnst │ arbeitEst│ heißt ←  │  (-s/-ß/-z stem: du-form loses the s)
 er/sie/es  │  -t    │ wohnt  │ arbeitEt │ heißt    │
 wir        │  -en   │ wohnen │ arbeiten │ heißen   │
 ihr        │  -t    │ wohnt  │ arbeitEt │ heißt    │
 sie / Sie  │  -en   │ wohnen │ arbeiten │ heißen   │
            └────────┴────────┴──────────┴──────────┘
                       e-insert after stem -t / -d / -n+consonant (arbeiten, finden, öffnen)

 Pronouns:   ich   du   er  sie  es   wir   ihr   sie   Sie
             I     you  he  she  it   we    you   they  you
                   (1 friend)           (2+ friends)    (formal, 1 or many, CAPITAL)
```

Three shapes to remember: **wir / sie / Sie = infinitive** · **er / ihr = -t** · **ich -e, du -st**.

## 2. The rule in words

**Find the stem**: cut `-en` (or `-n` after `-el/-er`: *sammeln → sammel-, wandern → wander-*). Add the ending for the person.

**Three "you"s.** `du` = one person you are on first-name terms with (family, friends, children, fellow students). `ihr` = two or more of those. `Sie` = anyone else, one or many: officials, shop staff, strangers, the exam examiner, the person you write a formal letter to. `Sie` takes the **same form as `sie` (they)**, i.e. the infinitive: *Sie wohnen, Sie arbeiten*. It is always capitalised.

**The e-insert.** If the stem ends in `-t`, `-d`, or a consonant + `-n/-m` (*arbeit-, find-, öffn-, atm-*), the endings `-st` and `-t` become `-est` and `-et`, so you can pronounce them: *du arbeitest, er findet, ihr öffnet*.

**The s-merge.** If the stem ends in `-s, -ß, -z, -x` (*heiß-, reis-, tanz-*), the `du` ending is just `-t`: *du heißt, du reist, du tanzt* (same as `er`).

**What the Präsens covers.** German has one present tense for four English ones: *ich arbeite* = I work / I am working / I do work / (with a time word) I will work. **There is no continuous form.** *Ich lerne gerade* = I am learning right now.

**`es`** is also the dummy subject: *Es regnet. Wie geht es? Es gibt …*

> 🇧🇷 **Portuguese contrast** — You already conjugate for person (*moro, moras, mora, moramos, moram*), so the idea is familiar; only the endings are new. `Sie` works like *o senhor / a senhora*: the polite form takes a 3rd-person shape. Unlike PT, **the pronoun is never dropped**: *Moro em Berlim* → ***Ich** wohne in Berlin*. Present continuous: PT *estou aprendendo* → just *ich lerne*.
> 🇪🇸 **Spanish contrast** — **Top trap for Spanish speakers:** Spanish is a pro-drop language (*Vivo en Madrid*), whereas in German **the subject pronoun is always obligatory** (*Ich wohne in Madrid*). Nor is there an *estar + gerundio* progressive (*Estoy estudiando → Ich lerne gerade*). For "you", map `du` to *tú*, `ihr` to *vosotros* (or informal Latin American *ustedes* for friends), and `Sie` (always capitalised, verb in `-en`) to formal *usted* AND *ustedes*.
> 🇫🇷 **French contrast** — Like French (*je, tu, il*), German **never drops the subject pronoun** (*Ich wohne in Berlin*), and has no continuous tense (*Je suis en train de travailler* → *Ich arbeite gerade*). However, French *vous* splits into **two** pronouns in German: **ihr** for two or more friends/family members (*ihr wohnt*), and **Sie** (capitalised, verb in `-en`) for formal singular or plural (*Sie wohnen*).
> 🇬🇧 **English contrast** — English speakers under-conjugate (*er wohn* instead of *er wohnt*) and try to build *I am learning* with *sein* (*ich bin lernen*) — wrong. One present tense only.

## 3. Watch out

- `sie` lowercase is *she* **or** *they*; the verb tells you: *sie wohnt* (she) / *sie wohnen* (they). `Sie` capital mid-sentence = formal *you*.
- `ihr` is a pronoun (*you* plural) **and** a possessive (*her / their*): *Ihr kommt* vs *ihr Auto*. Context and verb form decide.
- Do not add `-s` for `er` like English: *er wohnt*, never *er wohns*.
- Infinitive `-n` verbs: *sammeln → ich sammle* (the `e` before `l` drops in *ich*), *wandern → ich wandere*.
