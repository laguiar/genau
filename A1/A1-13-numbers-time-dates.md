## 2. The rule in words

**Numbers** are written as one word up to a million: *dreihundertfünfundzwanzig* (325). Units come **before** tens with *und*: 24 = *vierundzwanzig*. Watch the irregular ones: *sechzehn, siebzehn, sechzig, siebzig, dreißig*. *eins* becomes *ein-* inside numbers (*einundzwanzig, einhundert*) and *ein/eine* before nouns. Phone numbers are read digit by digit or in pairs. Prices: *3,50 €* = *drei Euro fünfzig*; decimal comma, thousands point (*1.500 €*).

**Ordinals** add *-te* (4th–19th) or *-ste* (20th+) and are declined like adjectives; irregular: *erste, dritte, siebte, achte*. In writing, a full stop after the numeral means "-th": *der 7. September* (read: *der siebte*), *am 7. September* (*am siebten*, Dativ ending *-n*).

**Clock time.** Official time uses 24 hours and *Uhr* between hours and minutes: *14:20 = vierzehn Uhr zwanzig*. Informal time uses 12 hours, *nach/vor*, *Viertel*, and **halb + the coming hour**: *halb neun* = 8:30, not 9:30. *Uhr* = o'clock/clock; *Stunde* = hour as a duration (*zwei Stunden*).

**Dates.** Day first: *7.9.2026*. *Heute ist der siebte September* (● with *sein*), *am siebten September* (■ after *am*). Months: *Januar, Februar, März, April, Mai, Juni, Juli, August, September, Oktober, November, Dezember* (all masculine). Days: *Montag, Dienstag, Mittwoch, Donnerstag, Freitag, Samstag/Sonnabend, Sonntag* (masculine). Parts of the day: *am Morgen, am Vormittag, am Mittag, am Nachmittag, am Abend* (masculine) but *in der Nacht* (feminine). Adverbs: *morgens, mittags, abends, nachts, montags* (= regularly, lowercase).

**Time words without preposition:** *heute, morgen, gestern, übermorgen, vorgestern, jetzt, bald, später, jeden Tag, nächste Woche, letztes Jahr, diesen Monat* (▲ time expressions).

> 🇧🇷 **Portuguese contrast** — PT says tens first (*vinte e quatro*); German says units first (*vierundzwanzig*) — the most frequent slip when writing numbers you hear. *halb neun*: PT *oito e meia* = 8:30 = *halb **neun*** (half **to** nine). PT *de manhã / à tarde / à noite* → *am Morgen / am Nachmittag / am Abend* but *in der Nacht*. PT *em maio* → *im Mai*; *na segunda* → *am Montag*; *às oito* → *um acht*.
> 🇪🇸 **Spanish contrast** — German numbers from 21 to 99 read the **units before the tens** (*veinticuatro* = 20+4 → *vierundzwanzig* = 4-and-20). Watch **`halb`** on the clock: Spanish *las ocho y media* (8:30) looks back at 8, whereas German **halb neun** (8:30 = "half towards nine") looks forward to the **next** hour! Time prepositions: *a las 8 → **um** 8 Uhr*; *el lunes → **am** Montag* (no article *el*, use `am`!); *en mayo / en verano → **im** Mai / **im** Sommer*; standalone years take **no preposition** (*en 2026 → **2026***).
> 🇫🇷 **French contrast** — German numbers from 21 to 99 put the units before the tens (*vierundzwanzig* = "four-and-twenty", like French *vingt et un* flipped). Watch **`halb`**: French *huit heures et demie* (8:30) looks back at 8, whereas German **halb neun** (8:30 = "half towards nine") looks forward to the next hour! Time prepositions: *à 8 heures → **um** 8 Uhr*; *lundi / le lundi → **am** Montag*; *en mai / en été → **im** Mai / **im** Sommer* (no preposition before a standalone year: *en 2026 → **2026***).
> 🇬🇧 **English contrast** — *at 8* = *um 8*; *on Monday* = *am Montag*; *in May* = *im Mai*; *half past eight* = *halb neun*; *a quarter to* = *Viertel vor*. 24-hour clock is normal in writing.

## 3. Watch out

- *halb zwei* = **1:30**. Always the hour that is coming.
- *sechzehn/sechzig* (no *-s*), *siebzehn/siebzig* (no *-en*), *dreißig* (ß).
- *am Wochenende* (singular, *das Wochenende* → *am*), *in der Nacht*, *um Mitternacht*.
- *am 7. September* reads *am siebten* — in writing you never see the *-n*, but if you write the word out, add it.
- *Uhr* vs *Stunde*: *Es ist 8 Uhr* / *Der Kurs dauert zwei Stunden*.
- *seit* (since, still ongoing) is A2-04; at A1 use *von … bis* and *ab*.
