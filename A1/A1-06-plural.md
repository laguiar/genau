## 1. The rule in one picture

```
 Plural article is ALWAYS  die      (die Kinder, die Frauen, die Autos)

 Pattern       Typical for                                Examples
 -(e)n         ~90 % of FEMININE nouns; -e nouns; -in→-innen  die Frau→Frauen, Lampe→Lampen, Freundin→Freundinnen
 -e  (¨)       most MASCULINE + many neuter one-syllable    der Tisch→Tische, Stuhl→Stühle, das Jahr→Jahre
 ¨-er          NEUTER one-syllable (+ a few masculine)      das Kind→Kinder, Haus→Häuser, Buch→Bücher, der Mann→Männer
 –  (¨)        masc./neut. in -el -en -er -chen -lein       der Lehrer→Lehrer, Apfel→Äpfel, das Mädchen→Mädchen
 -s            foreign words, -o/-a/-i/-y, abbreviations    das Auto→Autos, Handy→Handys, Sofa→Sofas, die CD→CDs

 ¨ = umlaut on a/o/u where possible (Stuhl→Stühle, Apfel→Äpfel, Haus→Häuser) — never on e/i
```

## 2. The rule in words

There is no single plural ending. There are **five patterns**, and gender plus ending tell you which is likely:

1. **Feminine → `-(e)n`.** *die Frau → Frauen, die Blume → Blumen, die Zeitung → Zeitungen, die Wohnung → Wohnungen*. Feminine person nouns in *-in* double the n: *die Lehrerin → Lehrerinnen*. Exceptions: a small group of one-syllable feminines takes `¨-e`: *die Hand → Hände, die Stadt → Städte, die Nacht → Nächte, die Wand → Wände, die Wurst → Würste, die Kuh → Kühe, die Maus → Mäuse*; and *die Mutter → Mütter, die Tochter → Töchter*.
2. **Masculine → `-e`, often with umlaut.** *der Tisch → Tische, der Tag → Tage, der Freund → Freunde, der Stuhl → Stühle, der Zug → Züge, der Kopf → Köpfe*. Masculine nouns in *-e* (the n-nouns) take `-n`: *der Junge → Jungen, der Kollege → Kollegen, der Name → Namen*.
3. **Neuter one-syllable → `¨-er`.** *das Kind → Kinder, das Haus → Häuser, das Buch → Bücher, das Land → Länder, das Bild → Bilder, das Glas → Gläser*. Some masculines too: *der Mann → Männer, der Wald → Wälder*. Other neuters take `-e`: *das Jahr → Jahre, das Spiel → Spiele, das Brot → Brote, das Heft → Hefte*.
4. **`-el, -en, -er, -chen, -lein` (masc./neut.) → no ending**, sometimes umlaut: *der Lehrer → Lehrer, das Zimmer → Zimmer, das Mädchen → Mädchen, der Apfel → Äpfel, der Vater → Väter, der Bruder → Brüder, der Garten → Gärten*.
5. **Foreign / vowel-final → `-s`.** *das Auto → Autos, das Hotel → Hotels, das Handy → Handys, der Park → Parks, das Café → Cafés, die E-Mail → E-Mails*.

Learn the plural **with** the noun: *das Kind, -er · die Frau, -en · der Apfel, ¨-*. Dictionaries write it exactly like this.

**Plural has no `ein`.** *Ich habe ein Kind → Ich habe – Kinder.* Negation: *keine Kinder*. Quantity words: *viele, einige, zwei, alle + plural*.

> 🇧🇷 **Portuguese contrast** — PT plural is almost always *-s*; the German *-s* plural is the **rarest** pattern, reserved for foreign words. PT speakers over-use it (~~die Kinds~~). Also PT *os/as* → one German plural article, *die*.
> 🇪🇸 **Spanish contrast** — In Spanish, almost every plural simply adds `-s` or `-es` (*casas, mujeres*). German has five plural patterns (`-e, -er, -(e)n, -s, —`) plus frequent umlauts (*das Haus → die Häuser, der Apfel → die Äpfel*). Feminine nouns (`die`) take `-(e)n` over 90 % of the time (*die Frau → die Frauen*), while the `-s` plural (*die Autos, die Fotos, die Sofas*) is reserved for foreign loanwords.
> 🇫🇷 **French contrast** — In French, the plural `-s` or `-x` is mostly silent and only heard on the article (*les enfants*). In German, every plural noun changes its spoken ending (`-e, -er, -(e)n, -s`) and often adds an umlaut (*das Haus → die Häuser*). The `-s` plural (*die Autos, die Büros, die Hotels*) is used mainly for foreign loanwords, not native German nouns.
> 🇬🇧 **English contrast** — Same trap: *-s* is not the default. But English keeps a few German-style plurals (*man/men, foot/feet, child/children*) that show the umlaut and *-er* idea.

## 3. Watch out

- Umlaut only on **a, o, u, au** → *ä, ö, ü, äu*. *das Kind → Kinder* (no umlaut possible), *das Haus → Häuser*.
- Some plurals change meaning or are irregular: *die Bank → Bänke* (benches) / *Banken* (banks); *das Wort → Wörter* (individual words) / *Worte* (words as speech); *der Bus → Busse*; *das Museum → Museen*; *das Thema → Themen*; *die Firma → Firmen*; *das Praktikum → Praktika*.
- Some nouns are plural-only: *die Eltern, die Leute, die Ferien, die Geschwister*. Some are singular-only: *das Obst, das Gemüse, das Geld, die Milch*.
- The plural takes an extra `-n` in the Dativ (*mit den Kindern*, A1-15), except after `-n` or `-s`.
