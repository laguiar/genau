## 1. The rule in one picture

```
 GENDER = the article. Never learn bare nouns — fuse article + noun as ONE stress unit:
   derTisch · dasBett · dieLampe     (fixed spatial order everywhere in Genau: m · n · f · pl)

 ●  Nominativ (subject / after "sein")
            m        n        f        pl
 definite   der      das      die      die
 indefinite ein      ein      eine     —  (no plural of "ein": just the noun, or "keine")
 negative   kein     kein     keine    keine

 4-STEP GENDER TRIAGE (check in order — resolves ~80 % of nouns!)
 1. Compound?    → LAST part decides (100 %): das Zimmer + der Schlüssel = DER Zimmerschlüssel
 2. Suffix /     der  ← -er (people/tools: Lehrer, Computer)  -ig  -ling  -ismus  -ant  -ist  -or
    nominalised  die  ← -ung  -heit  -keit  -schaft  -ion  -tät  -ik  -ur  -ei  -in  -ie  -enz  -age
                 das  ← -chen  -lein  -um  -ment  -ma  verb-as-noun (das Essen, beim Lesen)
 3. Sound /      der  ← male persons · days · months · seasons · weather (Regen, Schnee, Wind)
    meaning      die  ← -e (Lampe, Straße ~90 %) · female persons · numbers as nouns (die Drei)
                 das  ← -o · Ge-…(e) · young beings (Kind, Baby) · letters · colours · metals · cities
 4. Exception?   → Bind as chunk + plural + agreement phrase (der Termin, -e · einen Termin)
```

## 2. The rule in words

**Gender is attached to the word, not the object — and bare nouns block learning.** *Das Mädchen* (the girl) is neuter because of `-chen`; *die Sonne* is feminine, *der Mond* masculine. Psycholinguistic research shows that learning a bare noun first (`Tisch = table`) *blocks* the article from bonding to it in memory. Always encode and say the article **fused with** the noun and its plural — *der Tisch, -e* — and practise it inside a short agreement phrase (*ein alter Tisch → ich kaufe ihn*). Run new nouns through the **4-step triage** above (~80 % accurate), use the plural ([A1-06](A1-06-plural.md)) as a second clue, and save vivid imagery only for true rule-breakers (full triage, 3-anchor imagery guide, and ~60 telc core chunks in [shared/gender.md](../shared/gender.md)).

**`der / die / das` (definite)** = the listener knows which one: already mentioned, unique, or obvious. *Ich habe ein Auto. **Das** Auto ist alt.*

**`ein / eine` (indefinite)** = one of many, first mention, or a category: *Das ist **ein** Stuhl. Ich habe **eine** Frage.* There is **no plural** form: *Das sind – Stühle.* The negation *kein* has a plural: *Das sind **keine** Stühle.*

**No article (Nullartikel)** with: professions and roles after *sein/werden* (*Ich bin – Lehrer*), nationalities (*Er ist – Brasilianer*), languages (*Ich spreche – Deutsch*), materials and uncountables in general statements (*Ich trinke – Kaffee*), plural in general (*Ich habe – Kinder*), most cities and countries (*in – Berlin, aus – Brasilien*), fixed phrases (*zu Hause, nach Hause, zu Fuß*). Countries with an article: *die Schweiz, die Türkei, die USA (pl.), der Iran, die Niederlande (pl.)*.

**Compound nouns take the gender of the last part**: *das Zimmer + der Schlüssel = **der** Zimmerschlüssel; der Kaffee + die Tasse = **die** Kaffeetasse*.

**Female forms** add `-in`: *der Lehrer → die Lehrerin, der Arzt → die Ärztin, der Freund → die Freundin* (plural *-innen*).

> 🇧🇷 **Portuguese contrast** — You already learn nouns with *o/a*, so keep the habit. But PT gender is **not** a guide: *o sol → die Sonne, a lua → der Mond, o carro → das Auto, a mesa → der Tisch, o livro → das Buch, a casa → das Haus*. Trust the German ending, never the PT word. Like PT, professions take no article: *Sou professor* = *Ich bin Lehrer* (English *I am **a** teacher* misleads).
> 🇪🇸 **Spanish contrast** — Like Spanish (*el / la*), German grammatical gender is arbitrary, so learn every noun with `der / die / das` — never assume the Spanish gender transfers (*el sol → **die** Sonne, la luna → **der** Mond, la mesa → **der** Tisch*). Watch Greek-origin words in `-ma` (*el problema, el tema, el clima*): they are masculine in Spanish, but **neuter (`das Problem, das Thema, das Klima`)** in German! Like Spanish (*Soy profesor*), drop the article before professions after *sein* (*Ich bin Lehrer*).
> 🇫🇷 **French contrast** — Like French (*le / la*), German grammatical gender is arbitrary and must be learned with the noun — do not trust the French gender (*le soleil → **die** Sonne, la lune → **der** Mond, la table → **der** Tisch*). Watch French loanwords ending in **`-age`**: they are masculine in French (*le garage, l'étage*) but **feminine (`die`)** in German (*die Garage, die Etage*). Like French (*Il est médecin*), omit the article before professions and nationalities after *sein* (*Er ist Arzt*).
> 🇬🇧 **English contrast** — English has one *the* and one *a*. German has three of each and they change with case; the article carries most of the grammar of the noun phrase. Do not skip it.

## 3. Watch out

- *-e* nouns are feminine ~90 %, but some common ones are not: *der Name, der Junge, der Kollege, der Kunde, das Auge, das Ende, der Käse*.
- *-er* is masculine for people and tools, but *das Fenster, das Zimmer, die Mutter, die Schwester, die Butter, das Wasser* are common exceptions.
- Every noun is **capitalised**, including *das Essen, das Lesen* (verbs used as nouns) and *der Deutsche* (adjective used as noun).
- After *sein* the noun stays ● : *Das ist **ein** Tisch* (never *einen*).
